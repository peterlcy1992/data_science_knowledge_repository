**Google — Making AI Search Ask Better Questions, 20x Faster**

When an AI search system needs to answer a broad question, it often has to break that one question into several smaller, more specific searches — a trick called "query fan-out." The problem is doing that well is slow: today's models "think out loud" for tens of seconds before they even start searching, and just as often they generate several near-identical sub-questions instead of genuinely different angles. This episode covers how Google Research tackled both problems at once. We walk through their two-stage approach: first, training a small language model with reinforcement learning — specifically rewarded for producing sub-questions that are grounded, genuinely diverse, and true to the original request — to get really good at this fan-out task offline, where speed doesn't matter. Then, instead of shipping that slow-but-smart model to production, they use it to teach a completely different, much smaller model: a 54-million-parameter diffusion model that generates a whole set of sub-questions in one fast pass, with no step-by-step reasoning at all. We break down why borrowing diffusion — the technology behind AI image generators — for a text-search problem is such an unusual and clever choice, and what the result looks like in practice: search fan-out that used to take up to 50 seconds now finishes in a fraction of a second, without losing quality.
