# Amazon — How much history does a customer forecast need?

When you forecast how active a customer will be over the next year, what matters more: more data, a bigger model, a newer architecture, or simply letting the model see more of each customer's past? Amazon researchers ran a controlled, one-lever-at-a-time comparison across eight model families, from a one-feature linear model to transformers, recurrent networks and gradient-boosted trees. Doubling the observation window from 8 to 16 weeks lifted accuracy by 0.07 to 0.11 in log-space R² in every family. Ten times more customers, wider or deeper networks, and switching sequence architectures each moved it by less than 0.007. We walk through why a longer window quietly changes which customers get scored, and how matched cohorts show the raw comparison understates the gain. We also cover how the value of extra history saturates within weeks for heavy users but keeps growing for light ones, and why a time-aware positional encoding was the biggest model-side improvement. Then we look at the authors' unusual honesty: pre-registered predictions (including the one that failed), a voided calendar control, and why they call the result a robust association rather than a pure history effect. Source article: "The Window Lever: Longer Observation Windows Outweigh the Observed Effects of Data, Capacity, and Architecture in Customer Behavior Forecasting" — Amazon (arXiv cs.LG), https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26521 (published 2026-11).
