# Instacart — The $5 Rounding Rule Nobody Meant to Test

A deep dive into how Instacart's economics team found a causal answer hiding inside a mundane payments policy. Every Instacart order gets a temporary card-authorization hold slightly above the order total, rounded up to the nearest $5 — a rule that, by accident, created a natural experiment: orders just below a $5 threshold and orders just above it are essentially identical shoppers, except for how big a buffer they got. We cover the business problem (bigger buffers mean fewer follow-up authorizations, but also more card declines, and nobody knew where the real trade-off point was), the technique (regression discontinuity design, a quasi-experimental method that treats the threshold itself as if it were a coin flip), and the twist that makes this one stand out: Instacart didn't just trust the causal estimate — they ran a real follow-up A/B test that confirmed it, cutting declines and lifting order volume at the same time.
