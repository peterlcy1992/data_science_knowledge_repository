# Pinterest — A Cheap LLM Judge Plus Smarter Sampling Cuts Search-Experiment MDE

A deep dive into how Pinterest Search measures relevance in online experiments without waiting on human annotators. A fine-tuned multilingual cross-encoder (XLM-RoBERTa-large, trained on about 2.6 million human-labelled query-Pin pairs) scores relevance on a 5-point scale and agrees with humans exactly 73.7% of the time and within one level 91.7% of the time on US queries. Cheap labels then make it possible to redesign the sampling: paired control/treatment queries, strata by query interest and popularity, and Neyman optimal allocation. The authors report the minimum detectable effect falling from roughly 1.3-1.5% to 0.25% or less, with stratification cutting the standard deviation by 52% to 67%. Non-English agreement is weaker and text-only features are a limitation. Source article: "LLM-based Relevance Assessment for Web-Scale Search Evaluation at Pinterest" — arXiv (Han Wang, Alex Whitworth, Pak Ming Cheung, Zhenjie Zhang, Krishna Kamath), https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03764 (published 2025-09).
