# IBM Research — Why Your AI Agent Might Not Ace the Same Task Twice

Most AI agent benchmarks report one number: average accuracy across several runs of a task. But average accuracy hides something that matters a lot more in practice — whether the agent behaves the *same way* every time you ask it to do the exact same thing. An agent that's 80% accurate on average could be one that nails some tasks every single time and flips a coin on others, and you'd never know which from the headline number. This episode digs into a fix IBM Research built for that blind spot: instead of expensively re-running whole tasks over and over to check for flakiness, their system pinpoints the *specific moments* inside a single run where the agent's decision was genuinely up for grabs — moments that would tip a different way if you asked again — and turns those moments into explicit rules the agent follows the next time around. We cover how that detection works without needing any human-labeled "right answers," why the fix didn't cost the agent any of its average accuracy to gain reliability, and the concrete numbers from testing on a benchmark called AppWorld: nearly doubling the fraction of tasks the agent could nail on every single attempt, while the rules it learned also carried over to help on tasks it hadn't seen before.
