# Shopify — One generative model for search and recommendations

Shopify has to retrieve relevant products from a changing catalog of billions of items, in tens of milliseconds, for both search and recommendations. Its SID-LLM turns both jobs into generating short "semantic IDs" — three codewords quantized from each product's embedding — from a language model jointly pre-trained on catalog and shopping-behaviour data. We walk through the design choices that trade quality against latency (codebook sizes, a 2.6B-parameter backbone chosen over larger Qwen3 models, multi-token prediction plus a lightweight ranker distilled from an LLM relevance judge), the popularity-weighted trick for turning an ID back into products, and how Shopify measures what a retriever actually adds: an LLM-judged relevance score with a fixed normalizer, and "incremental pool recall", where SID-LLM adds 2.65 points to the candidate pool, 61% more than the next-best generator. On its own it comes close to Shopify's whole multi-model search stack, and in a home-feed A/B test it raised attributed orders by 7.95%. Source article: "Generative Search and Recommendation at Shopify" — arXiv cs.IR (Shopify: Kangaspunta, Hajji, Fofadiya et al.), https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08804 (published 2026-11).
