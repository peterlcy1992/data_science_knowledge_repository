# Netflix — Do better recommendations make hits bigger?

Do recommender systems funnel everyone toward the same few hits, or spread viewing into the long tail? Netflix researchers answer with a 60-day experiment on more than 8.5 million subscribers. A control group kept the recommender frozen as it was before the test, while everyone else got every personalization improvement shipped in that window. The improvements moved recommendations and plays away from the top 5% "superstar" titles and mostly toward moderately popular "middle-tail" titles, with little change for the most niche ones. Concentration fell: the Herfindahl index of recommendations dropped 5.7% and of plays 1.2%. Viewing still rose, with view hours up 0.37%, distinct titles played up 1.2%, and more first plays coming from the homepage. We walk through how a cumulative holdback works and why bucket shares and concentration indices sit alongside engagement metrics. We also cover how the authors check match quality with completion rates, thumbs and Lee bounds, and their simple model of why better algorithms weaken popularity bias most for the middle tail. We close on what a bundled treatment can't tell you and what this means for which content is worth making as algorithms mature. Source article: "Recommendation Quality and the Concentration of Consumption: Experimental Evidence from Netflix" — Netflix (arXiv econ.GN / cs.IR), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21274 (published 2026-08).
