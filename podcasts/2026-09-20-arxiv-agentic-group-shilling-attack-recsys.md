# AI Research — How a Coordinated AI Swarm Rigs Your Recommendations

A deep dive into AGAS, a new attack that uses a coordinated swarm of AI agents to manipulate what a recommendation system shows you. Instead of one bot spamming fake reviews, a central "Coordinator" directs a team of role-switching worker agents — some probing how the system reacts, some quietly building a believable rating history, and one, only once fully trusted, delivering the actual push for the target product. We cover the business problem (fake-profile "shilling" attacks are getting cheaper and more humanlike thanks to AI agents), the technical approach (the Coordinator's adaptive strategies, the workers' four rotating roles, and why going dormant on purpose is itself a tactic), and the results: the attack was dramatically more effective at promoting a target item, far better at evading existing fraud detectors, and vastly cheaper to run than prior AI-driven attacks — while barely disturbing the experience for everyone else on the platform. Source article: "An Efficient and Effective Agentic Group Shilling Attack on Recommender Systems" — arXiv (academic), https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09551 (published 2026-09).
