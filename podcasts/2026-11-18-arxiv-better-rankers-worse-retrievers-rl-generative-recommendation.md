# HKUST & Trip.com — When RL makes a recommender worse at retrieval

Reinforcement learning has become the standard second step for generative recommenders that "write out" the ID of a user's next item, and it reliably improves the first ten results. But most of these models are deployed as retrieval stages that hand hundreds of candidates to a ranker — so what happens further down the list? This paper from HKUST and Trip.com Group scores every catalogue item for every test user and finds that, on all five Amazon datasets studied, GRPO post-training makes the model a better ranker and a worse retriever: on Office, NDCG@10 rises from 11.59% to 12.55% while recall at 500 falls by 5.1 points, and 14–22% of the items the original model had in its top 500 drop out. We walk through the diagnosis — why the objective itself isn't to blame, how penalising the model's sampled alternatives, which are often other users' real next items, spreads to similar users, and the controlled experiments that pin it down, including a score computed before training that predicts which users lose the most. Then the fix: a single "out-of-rollout anchoring" loss term that keeps the original model's ordering of items RL didn't sample, preserving the top-of-list gain and restoring recall to the pre-RL level on all five datasets. We close on why every funnel stage should be evaluated at the depth it is actually used, and what the paper leaves open at industrial scale. Source article: "Better Rankers, Worse Retrievers: The Hidden Cost of RL Post-Training in Generative Recommendation" — HKUST / Trip.com Group (arXiv cs.IR), https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22838 (published 2026-11).
