# Meta — Predicting the full-scale winner from small training runs

Testing a recommender-model change properly means retraining at full scale, and a single cheap small-scale run can point the wrong way: adding movie genre information to an LLM-based recommender hurt hit rate at small scale but helped at full scale. Meta researchers show that models with a change applied still follow a power-law scaling curve of the same form, with different coefficients, so a sweep of small runs can be extrapolated, with intervals, into the probability that the change beats the baseline by a chosen margin at full scale. We cover the Bayesian decision rule (more than 90% probability of a 1% loss improvement), why a sweep at one-tenth to one-half scale costs about 55% of one full run, results across more than 40 changes on Wukong, HSTU and an LLM-based recommender, and where the power-law assumption breaks. Source article: "Exploiting Feature-invariant Scaling Laws for Efficient A/B Experimentation in Recommendation Systems" — arXiv cs.IR (Meta: Zhiliang Chen, Sammy Nammari, Jiahao Su, Xiaoqiang Luo), https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13539 (published 2026-11).
