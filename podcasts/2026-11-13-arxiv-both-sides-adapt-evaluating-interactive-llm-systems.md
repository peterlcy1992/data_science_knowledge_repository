# Columbia University — Why LLM evals and A/B tests can disagree

People change what they ask a chatbot after seeing its answers, so a model partly chooses the prompts it gets judged on. This paper shows that an offline benchmark and a live A/B test therefore measure different things: a "common-distribution effect" (both models on the same prompts) versus an "induced-distribution effect" (each model on the prompts its own users end up sending). We walk through the email-writing example where a model that is better on every task still loses the A/B test, because its stronger answers push users toward harder requests. We also cover the split of an A/B difference into a performance effect and a composition effect, and a sufficient condition, checkable from offline and historical data, for when a benchmark ranking is guaranteed to match the live test. Then come the side effects of user adaptivity: a new route to reward hacking, novelty effects when users have learned to avoid a task, and a stylized model in which feedback between users and the model inflates estimator variance. Source article: "When Both Sides Adapt: The Challenges of Evaluating Interactive LLM Systems" — arXiv stat.ME (Hannah Li), https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17239 (published 2026-11).
