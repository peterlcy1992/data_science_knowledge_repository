# Meta — The Agent That Writes Its Own GPU Kernels

A deep dive into KernelEvolve, the system Meta built to close a widening gap: the number of GPU and accelerator kernels a company needs optimized grows multiplicatively across hardware generations, model architectures, and operator types, while expert kernel engineers — the people who could tune them by hand — don't scale nearly as fast. We cover the business problem (thousands of configurations across NVIDIA and AMD GPUs, Meta's own MTIA chips, and CPUs, each needing its own hand-tuned kernel), the technical approach (an LLM synthesizer that writes candidate kernels across multiple languages, a Monte Carlo tree search engine that explores hundreds of variants per kernel instead of generating just one, a knowledge base that compounds what it learns across sessions, and a final stage that turns successful optimization runs into training data for smaller specialist models), and the realized payoff: over 60% inference throughput improvement on NVIDIA GPUs for a production ads model, over 25% training throughput improvement on Meta's own chips, and kernel optimization work that used to take weeks of expert time now running in hours.
