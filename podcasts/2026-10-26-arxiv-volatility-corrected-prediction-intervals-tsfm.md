# Time-Series Foundation Models — Keeping Forecast Intervals Honest When Volatility Shifts

A deep dive into new work from Cho and Matteson on a quiet failure of probabilistic forecasts: intervals can hit their nominal marginal coverage on average while still miscovering during high-volatility stretches, because forecast errors are serially dependent and heteroskedastic. The authors call this local scale miscalibration and document it in the native intervals of time-series foundation models (Chronos, Moirai, TimesFM). Their fix, Volatility-Corrected Prediction Intervals, is post-hoc: it models the volatility of standardized forecast residuals with a GARCH model and applies a multiplicative correction to the forecaster's own predictive scale. Across four real-world datasets it gives the lowest rolling coverage error against conformal baselines at comparable interval width, with stated limits at longer horizons and for tail coverage. Source article: "Volatility-Corrected Prediction Intervals: Locally Adaptive Uncertainty Quantification for Probabilistic Time-Series Forecasting" — arXiv stat.ME (Cho & Matteson), https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31621 (published 2026-10).
