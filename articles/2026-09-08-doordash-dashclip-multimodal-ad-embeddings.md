# DashCLIP: Leveraging Multimodal Models for Generating Semantic Embeddings for DoorDash

**Source:** [DoorDash / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07110) · Published 2025-04 · Added 2026-09-08
**Category:** Personalization & Recommender Systems · **Tags:** `multimodal`, `contrastive-learning`, `clip`, `ads-retrieval`, `semantic-embeddings`, `llm-curated-data`

## TL;DR

DoorDash built DashCLIP, a two-encoder contrastive framework that jointly aligns product images, product text, and user search queries in one embedding space to power personalized ads retrieval, ranking, and relevance — training its query encoder on an LLM-curated relevance dataset instead of relying on user-engagement history, and beating CLIP, BLIP, and FLAVA baselines offline while delivering a reported CTR and CVR uplift after deployment.

## 1. Business context

DoorDash's ads systems need a shared representation that can compare what a product *is* (its image and text) against what a user *wants* (a free-text search query), across ads retrieval, ranking, and relevance scoring. Off-the-shelf multimodal models like CLIP are trained on general web image-text pairs, not DoorDash's specific catalog and query distribution, and they don't capture the nuanced entity relationships (cuisine, ingredients, dietary attributes, brand) that matter for food and retail delivery search. Building a domain-specific embedding space that multiple downstream ads use cases can share — rather than each team building its own bespoke representation — was the goal.

## 2. Technical details

DashCLIP is a two-stage contrastive framework built around two encoders:

- **Product encoder** — a uni-modal and multi-modal encoder aligned through contrastive learning over DoorDash's own image-text product data, learning a joint embedding for each product's photo and description.
- **Query encoder** — trained on an **LLM-curated relevance dataset** rather than historical click/engagement data. This is the paper's key departure from typical two-tower ads retrieval setups: instead of learning what users *have* clicked (which is biased toward already-popular items and struggles with cold-start products), the query-product relevance labels are generated by an LLM judging semantic relevance directly, giving the encoder a training signal that isn't dependent on — or biased by — historical engagement volume.

At inference, a user's query is embedded and a K-nearest-neighbor search against the product embedding space produces a ranked candidate list for downstream selection (retrieval), which then feeds ranking and relevance-scoring stages. The paper reports offline comparisons against CLIP, BLIP, and FLAVA as baselines, with DashCLIP outperforming all three; the source material available did not surface the exact offline metric deltas (e.g., precision@k or recall@k figures), so those specific numbers are omitted here rather than guessed.

## 3. Impact — potential & realized

**Realized:** offline evaluation showed DashCLIP outperforming CLIP, BLIP, and FLAVA baselines on relevance and categorization tasks. After deployment, DoorDash reported a "significant uplift" in click-through rate and conversion rate for personalized ads recommendations — the exact percentages were not available in the sources reviewed.

**Potential:** because the embeddings are trained to be general-purpose (not tied to one specific ranking model or use case), DoorDash designed them to be reused across retrieval, ranking, and relevance simultaneously, and the LLM-curated training signal specifically targets the query-product cold-start problem — new products with little or no engagement history still get a meaningful embedding, since their representation comes from content rather than accumulated clicks.

## Claude's Take

> Opinion, not the source's claims.

### Novelty: 4/5 — The LLM-curated query encoder is the real contribution

The multimodal contrastive-alignment part of DashCLIP (image + text → joint embedding) is well-trodden ground — CLIP-style architectures are now standard in e-commerce search and ads. What's genuinely interesting is training the *query* encoder on LLM-curated relevance judgments instead of engagement logs: it directly targets the two classic weaknesses of engagement-based training (popularity bias and cold-start blindness) without needing a separate cold-start model. That's a clean, transferable idea for any marketplace where query-product engagement data is sparse or skewed, which is why this scores higher than a typical "we fine-tuned CLIP on our catalog" write-up.

### Jargon buster

- **Contrastive learning** — a training method that pulls matching pairs (e.g., a product's image and its correct description) closer together in embedding space while pushing mismatched pairs apart, without needing explicit relevance labels for every pair.
- **CLIP / BLIP / FLAVA** — established general-purpose multimodal (image+text) embedding models used here as baselines; each uses a different architecture and training recipe for aligning vision and language.
- **Two-tower model** — an architecture with two separate encoders (here, query and product) that map inputs into the same embedding space, allowing fast similarity search (e.g., K-nearest-neighbor) instead of scoring every pair jointly.
- **Cold-start** — the problem of making good recommendations or ad matches for new products or queries that have little or no historical engagement data to learn from.
