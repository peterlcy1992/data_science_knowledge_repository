# AI-assisted design and analysis of experiments with unstructured treatments

**Source:** [arXiv stat.ME (Ben-Michael & Branson)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33818) · Published 2026-10 · Added 2026-10-27
**Category:** experimentation-causal · **Tags:** `experiment-design`, `unstructured-treatments`, `internal-external-validity`, `generative-ai`, `model-assisted-estimation`, `finite-population`

## TL;DR

In randomized experiments where the treatment is a text, image or video, the effect of an interpretable attribute can be confounded by correlated attributes (internal validity) and may not generalize beyond the stimuli used (external validity). The paper designs how treatments are sampled, using generative AI or a large database, to trade the two off, with conservative inference and a calibrated AI-assisted estimator.

## 1. Business context

Social-science experiments increasingly randomize subjects to text, images, audio or video, but the target is usually a structured attribute (a linguistic or visual cue), not the specific stimuli. A representative sample of stimuli reproduces natural correlations between the attribute and other features, which confounds the attribute's effect; carefully constructed stimuli (the paper cites a study with four professionally produced videos) may not generalize. The same tension shows up in any test of a message, creative or UI-copy attribute.

## 2. Technical details

Working in a finite-population framework where treatment assignment is the only randomness, the authors treat AI generations and predictions as black boxes with no performance guarantees. Subjects are assigned treatments drawn from design distributions for treatments with and without the focal attribute. The bias of the difference-in-means estimator is governed by the discrepancy between (i) the design distributions with and without the attribute, (ii) design and target distributions, and (iii) design and natural distributions; an optimization procedure finds design distributions that minimize this bias, with a user-set weight on internal versus external validity. The design extends to AI manipulation of treatments (adding or removing the attribute). For analysis they give conservative asymptotic inference for difference-in-means and a calibrated AI-assisted estimator that uses zero-shot AI predictions of outcomes to improve precision without introducing bias.

## 3. Impact — potential & realized

Realized (illustrations, as reported; both experiments are hypothetical, evaluated with semi-synthetic simulation): a text example on 21,630 Reddit and Gab comments (Qian et al. 2019) with profanity as the focal attribute (42% of comments contain profanity) and 768-dimensional MPNet embeddings as features; an image example using congressional portraits and AI-manipulated versions. The paper states that the optimal design distributions help mitigate feature differences induced by imperfect AI generations. Limits the authors list: the focal attribute is assumed binary, pre-specified, single and measured without error; unstructured covariates and outcomes are out of scope. Potential: an inference-time procedure for steering AI-generated stimuli sampling toward lower bias in experiments on messages and creatives. I did not find real-experiment results in the paper.

## Claude's Take

> Opinion, not the source's claims.

### Novelty: 4/5 — Brings explicit design-stage control of the internal/external validity trade-off to experiments whose treatments are generated by AI.

This is the experimentation counterpart of 'LLM-generated content as treatment': it says do not just sample whatever the generator gives you, choose the sampling distribution to cap confounding from correlated attributes. Teams testing copy or imagery attributes can borrow the framing even without the full optimizer. Evidence is simulation-only, and results depend on how good the feature embeddings are at capturing the confounding attributes.

### Jargon buster

- **Unstructured treatment** — A treatment that is raw content (text, image, video) rather than a few structured levels.
- **Focal attribute** — The interpretable feature of the stimulus whose causal effect is the target.
- **Finite-population framework** — Inference where the subjects and potential outcomes are fixed and the only randomness is the random assignment.
- **Model-assisted (calibrated) estimator** — An estimator that uses black-box predictions to reduce variance while a calibration step keeps it unbiased.
