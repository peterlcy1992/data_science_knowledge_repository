## 🔬 Deep Dive: Variance reduction combining pre-experiment and in-experiment data

**Source:** [CLeaR 2026 / PMLR 323 (Zhexiao Lin, UC Berkeley; Pablo Crespo, Etsy)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v323/lin26a.html) · Published 2026 · Category: Experimentation & Causal Inference (Core DS)

*(Core DS was due in the rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS.)*

**Business.** A/B test sensitivity is capped by traffic. CUPED/CUPAC use only pre-experiment data, which is weak for new users and for session-driven outcomes. In-experiment (post-treatment) variables are usually avoided for fear of post-treatment bias, though they are often far more predictive.

**Technical.** Stage one is unchanged CUPAC (a LightGBM prediction from 117 pre-treatment covariates). Stage two adds a linear adjustment on post-treatment covariates that satisfy mean equivalence, E[Z|W=1]=E[Z|W=0]. The condition is testable, unlike surrogacy or principal-ignorability assumptions. The authors derive asymptotic normality and variance estimators. Candidates are screened with two-sample tests across arms (Holm/Bonferroni, or TOST equivalence tests for very large samples).

**Impact.** On 29 Etsy experiments (primary metric: conversion rate), 23 post-treatment covariates gave comparable or greater variance reduction than CUPAC with 117 pre-treatment covariates, with a predictive-accuracy gain (difference in sqrt(R²)) of 0.02 to above 0.14. Aggregate percentages are only in a figure and are not quoted. Validity depends on mean equivalence holding, so treatments that could shift a screened covariate need ongoing diagnostics.

**Claude's Take (4/5).** The novelty is the discipline rather than the regression: a testable, platform-level screen that separates balanced post-treatment variables from mediators.
