## Deep dive: GenPage: Towards End-to-End Generative Homepage Construction at Netflix

**Source:** [arXiv preprint, accepted RecSys 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31031) · Published 2026-06 · Novelty 4/5

Netflix's homepage has always been assembled by a multi-stage recommender pipeline — one system for candidate retrieval, another for per-item ranking, a third for the layout logic that decides which rows appear and in what order. Each stage is its own model with its own training loop, and stitching them together into one coherent, structured homepage adds both engineering complexity and serving latency. GenPage's bet is that a single generative model, trained end-to-end to produce the whole homepage at once, can replace this pipeline outright — while still handling the real production constraints a research prototype could ignore: cold start for new members and titles, freshness as the catalog shifts, business-rule placement requirements, and a tight latency budget.

**How it works.** GenPage treats homepage construction as sequence generation: the user's history and request context become a prompt, and a transformer autoregressively generates the full structured, multi-row homepage as its response — one forward pass replaces what used to take multiple coordinated model calls. Training follows an LLM-style recipe: pretraining on historical production homepages to learn what a well-formed page looks like, then post-training via either weighted binary classification (a supervised approach reweighting examples by engagement outcome) or reinforcement learning (optimizing the generation policy directly against a reward signal). Two offline findings stand out — enriching the prompt with richer user/catalog context beat simply scaling up model capacity in their current regime, and RL post-training increased homepage diversity as an emergent side effect even though diversity was never an explicit part of the reward.

**What actually happened.** In online A/B testing against Netflix's existing production system, GenPage delivered a substantial lift on the core engagement metric while cutting end-to-end serving latency by 20% — a case where collapsing a multi-model pipeline into one generative model improved quality and speed at the same time, rather than trading one for the other.

**Claude's take (novelty 4/5):** Treating an entire structured homepage as one autoregressive generation target — rather than composing it from separately-trained retrieval, ranking, and layout models — is a genuinely different production architecture, not a routine model swap, and the simultaneous latency-and-engagement win is the strongest evidence of real advance here. It lands at 4 rather than 5 because the core idea (sequence-to-sequence generation replacing multi-stage pipelines) follows a pattern already emerging in generative retrieval and generative ads ranking elsewhere in industry; Netflix's contribution is applying it convincingly to the harder, more structurally constrained homepage-layout problem.
