## 🔎 Deep dive: How Different are Different Differences-in-Differences? A Large-Scale Meta-Analysis

*arXiv econ.EM (Peter Hull, Brown University) · experimentation-causal · Core DS pick (rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS, so Core DS was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** In staggered-adoption difference-in-differences, the workhorse two-way fixed effects (TWFE) regression is now understood to carry a "negative weights" concern: it can implicitly apply non-convex weights to heterogeneous treatment effects, so even if all effects are positive the TWFE estimand can be negative. Whether that matters in real analyses is an empirical question.

**The method.** Hull uses agentic AI to search 16,094 papers (2000–2026) in ten general-interest economics journals for staggered-adoption DiD specifications with public replication archives, giving 1,346 replicated specifications from 108 papers. Each is estimated with TWFE and with two modern estimators: DiD-averaging (Callaway–Sant'Anna style, prespecified convex weights) and imputation (Borusyak et al. style). Hierarchical models separate estimation noise from true differences between estimands.

**Results.** TWFE and modern estimates are correlated around 90%. About 14.5% of TWFE estimates change sign under DiD-averaging and 10.8% under imputation, but only 3.5% and 2.2% are sign flips with a statistically significant difference. Modern estimators' standard errors are on average 47% (DiD-averaging) and 14% (imputation) larger than TWFE's. Hierarchical models imply a 91–98% correlation between estimands and a 0.6–2.1% probability of a significantly opposite-signed TWFE estimate. Reversals are less common in 2021+ papers and in papers reporting both estimators. The paper notes sign flips can also come from parallel-trends failures, which it does not assess.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** A rare audit of a theory-driven scare: "check, don't panic". Report a modern estimator beside TWFE and expect wider standard errors. The sample is published economics papers with public replication archives, so generalising to product-analytics rollouts is an inference, not a result.
