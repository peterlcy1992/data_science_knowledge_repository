## 🔎 Deep Dive: Agentic Machine Learning Modeling at Instacart

**Source:** [Instacart Tech Blog](https://tech.instacart.com/agentic-machine-learning-modeling-at-instacart-fb3ecd295ee7) · Published 2026-09
**Category:** LLMs & Generative AI

### TL;DR

Instacart's ML engineers ran coding agents on real modeling work — feature exploration, architecture choices, tuning — not just glue code, including on some of the company's most mature, already-optimized production models. The headline finding: agents proved themselves competent modeling collaborators, with the most promising runs delivering 3–5% offline improvements in held-out error, alongside honest safety and process lessons about giving agents that much latitude.

### Business context

Applied ML teams have mostly absorbed AI-assisted coding into the software-engineering parts of their job. What's less settled is whether agents can meaningfully help with the actual *modeling* work — picking features, trying architectures, tuning a model a team has already spent months optimizing by hand. That's a harder bar precisely because mature production models are the case with the least low-hanging fruit left. Instacart's ML engineering team set out to see whether agentic tools could productively take on that exploration work across a variety of model types and business contexts.

### Technical details

The team let agents explore feature sets, modeling approaches, and tuning choices against production baselines — deliberately including some of Instacart's most mature, already-optimized models, since that's the hardest possible test. The write-up frames this as a shift in the modeling loop itself: agents as genuine exploration collaborators, not just code generators around models humans design. Critically, agent-produced gains aren't trusted at face value — any offline improvement has to clear a battery of randomization checks before it's credible enough to take to a full online A/B test, a discipline the team argues matters *more*, not less, when the "researcher" proposing a change is a model that can generate a plausible-looking result without a human's instinct for spurious correlations.

### Impact

**Realized:** some of the agent-assisted runs delivered 3–5% offline improvements in held-out mean absolute error (MAE) against the strongest baseline, among the most promising results, on already-mature models. Across a range of model types and business contexts, agents functioned as competent modeling collaborators.

**Potential:** if agentic tools can reliably extend a modeling team's exploration budget — more features, architectures, and tuning combinations than a human researcher has time for — that changes the shape of applied ML work generally. But the post is equally clear about the flip side: broad agent permissions in a modeling environment are a real safety surface, and the team explicitly recommends sandboxing an agent's environment even though their own exercises only hit minor problems. There's also a compliance callout — an agent exploring model changes may not know about legal or contractual limitations a human modeler would already respect.

### Claude's Take

> Opinion, not the source's claims.

**Novelty: 3/5 — a believable, honestly-reported early result, not a breakthrough.** Pointing coding agents at modeling exploration instead of just surrounding code is an obvious next step now that agentic coding tools are mainstream, and Instacart isn't claiming otherwise. What makes this worth reading is the honesty about the bar being "some of the most promising runs," not every run, on models chosen specifically because they were already hard to improve — plus a genuine safety callout instead of a pure win narrative.

Similar/related work: Auto-RecSys: Harnessing Autonomous Research Agents for Industry-Scale Recommender Systems (a more architecturally elaborate take on the same idea at Meta); NetEase's AutoLR (another agentic research-to-launch pipeline, with a notable self-audited failure mode worth reading alongside Instacart's own safety concerns); the broader AutoML/Bayesian-optimization literature this work is effectively extending with an LLM-driven search process.
