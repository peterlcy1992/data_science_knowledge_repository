## 🔎 Deep dive: Baseline and Incremental: User, Item, and Request Debiasing in Instagram Feed Ranking

*arXiv cs.IR (Chen et al., Meta), Oct 2026 · search-ranking · novelty 4/5*
Why this pick: AI/ML-systems was due (2 of the last 4 deep dives were Core DS, so rotation goes to AI), and this is the strongest un-dived AI entry, with a Core-DS flavour: an ablation-based measurement argument.

🎙️ Deep-dive podcast: podcasts/2026-10-21-arxiv-instagram-feed-baseline-incremental-debiasing.m4a
🎙️ Episode S1E43 — cover ✓, RSS draft ✓ (unpublished; publish from the RSS.com dashboard)

**Business context.** Feed rankers score candidates with a weighted sum of engagement predictions. Each prediction mixes a baseline (what this viewer, item or request would produce anyway) with the incremental lift from showing the candidate. Most prior work removes the baseline; the authors argue that discards signal.

**How it works.** Viewer-habit, item-popularity and request-context debiasing become instances of one parameterized operator, with a design procedure to pick its setting. The value model keeps the raw prediction and adds the incremental part as a separately weighted term; media-type bias is handled by a debiased label. Evaluation is a four-arm production ablation (neither term, each alone, both). Formulas, weights and datasets are not in the abstract.

**Impact (as reported).** At production weights the two-term arm beat both single-term arms on point estimates in 11 of 13 judged (term, metric) pairs; of 26 direct contrasts on impressions, 17 significantly favoured it and 1 significantly favoured the opposing arm. Terms have run in Instagram Feed for most of a year and have been adopted on other Meta surfaces and both ranking stages. Effect sizes and confidence levels are not given.

**Claude's Take.** Treating the baseline as signal, not just bias, is simple and testable, and the paper reports its ablation candidly (11 of 13, one contrary contrast). Caveats: abstract-level read, 26 authors with no effect sizes shown, and many contrasts so the 17/26 headline needs multiplicity context.
