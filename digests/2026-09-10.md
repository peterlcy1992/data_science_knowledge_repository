## Deep dive: Build a Physical AI Model Factory with NVIDIA Cosmos 3 on SageMaker HyperPod

**Source:** [AWS Machine Learning Blog / NVIDIA](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/build-a-physical-ai-model-factory-with-nvidia-cosmos-3-on-sagemaker-hyperpod/) · Published 2026 · Novelty 4/5

Physical AI systems — robots, autonomous vehicles — don't get trained once and shipped; they need a perpetual improvement loop that ingests real-world data, generates synthetic variations to cover edge cases, post-trains updated models, and evaluates them in simulation before redeployment. The trouble is that pipeline traditionally fragments across separate GPU clusters per stage, creating expensive data movement and provisioning churn as demand shifts between stages. This AWS/NVIDIA post is a reference architecture for running the entire loop on one shared, persistent cluster instead — and it's paired with a genuinely elegant model design that makes the sharing possible.

**How it works.** NVIDIA Cosmos 3 carries all modalities — video, images, actions, audio, text — through a single unified token stream, and pairs two expert transformer towers (a reasoner and a generator) via per-layer dual-stream attention in a Mixture-of-Transformers design, rather than bolting separately trained components together. The genuinely clever part is **train-inference asymmetry**: training runs full denoising and video decoding, while a deployed policy runs only a handful of denoise steps and skips video decoding entirely for speed. Because of this, the *same checkpoint* can operate in three roles just by changing which tokens start as noise — as a **world model** generating synthetic video, as an **inverse-dynamics labeler** auto-labeling unlabeled video with actions, or as a deployed **policy** controlling a robot or vehicle. Three tiers ship: Cosmos3-Nano (16B, on an 8B Qwen3-VL backbone, fully fine-tuned), Cosmos3-Super (64B, on 32B Qwen3-VL, adapted via rank-16 LoRA), and Cosmos3-Edge (4B, for on-device use). On the infrastructure side, one persistent SageMaker HyperPod/EKS cluster shares a single GPU node pool (p5en.48xlarge, 8x H200 GPUs) across all pipeline stages, with unified FSx for Lustre storage and EFA interconnect — time-shared capacity instead of dedicated, often-idle per-stage pools.

**What actually happened.** The post reports a reproducible "goodput methodology" rather than leaderboard numbers: strong-scaling efficiency for the Super-LoRA workload measured 0.97–0.99 of linear from 1 to 4 nodes (per-step variance within ~3%); Model FLOPs Utilization ran ~0.50 for the 64B Super workload and ~0.24 for the lighter Nano vision workload against H200 BF16 peak; checkpoint overhead came out to roughly 1% of wall-clock at ~40-minute intervals; and node-failure cost is bounded to at most one checkpoint interval of redone work plus reschedule latency. The authors are explicit that these are relative, setup-specific figures meant to reproduce in shape on comparable hardware — "not a leaderboard number" — and they release dashboard and methodology code so other teams can measure their own clusters the same way.

**Claude's take (novelty 4/5):** The shared-cluster infrastructure pattern is sound but incremental engineering practice. What's genuinely novel is Cosmos 3 itself: using one checkpoint as world model, auto-labeler, and deployed policy purely by changing which tokens are noised elegantly unifies three roles that are normally separate models trained separately. Combined with rigorous, reproducible scaling measurements rather than cherry-picked headline numbers, this earns a 4 — not a 5, since Mixture-of-Transformers and token-stream unification both build on an active, fast-moving body of world-model research rather than starting from nothing.
