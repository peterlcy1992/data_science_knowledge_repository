## 🔎 Deep dive: Bayesian Causal Forests for Zero-Inflated Continuous Outcomes

*arXiv stat.ML (Souto, Wang & Shuryak, Oct 2026) · statistical-modeling · novelty 3/5*
Why this pick: Core DS was due (only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS), and this is the freshest solid Core-DS entry.

🎙️ Podcast: skipped (NotebookLM not configured in this environment)

**Business context.** Many outcomes (spend, usage days, adverse-event days) have a point mass at zero (about 25% of patients here) plus a skewed positive tail. HTE tools often ignore the zero mass, assume fragile parametric forms, or add noise through data augmentation.

**How it works.** ZIC-BCF-Smear decouples the problem: a Probit Bayesian Causal Forest on everyone models whether the outcome is non-zero; a Gaussian BCF on the active subgroup models positive intensity. Subpopulation Propensity Adjustment handles regularization-induced confounding in the active group, and Duan's smearing maps predictions back to the original scale without parametric scale factors. Abstract-level read.

**Impact (as reported).** In simulations it matches correctly specified parametric models without their assumptions and stays calibrated on nonlinear data where competitors diverge or over-cover (no specific metrics given on the page). On the SPECTRUM phase III head-and-neck trial (panitumumab + chemo, days with severe toxicity) it estimates about +10 days per patient, concentrated in the positive-intensity component.

**Claude's Take.** Mostly a careful recombination of known pieces (BCF, two-part models, smearing); the practical value is the handling of regularization bias in the active subgroup. The clinical application involves differential treatment duration, so it isn't a clean causal contrast for product use.
