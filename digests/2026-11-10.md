## 🔎 Deep dive: Knowing When to Intervene: Causal Evaluation of AI Agents

*arXiv cs.AI (Sawarni, Xing, Zrnic, Johari, Syrgkanis; Stanford) · llm-genai (AI/ML, tied to DS measurement) · AI/ML pick (rotation: the last 4 deep dives included 2 Core DS, so AI/ML-systems was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** Agent benchmarks report success or failure, but deployers must decide mid-trajectory whether to review a proposed tool call, steer the agent, or screen adversarial content. The authors treat that as a causal question: does intervening at this point improve the final outcome? It is a dynamic treatment problem, since an intervention can change later states and intervention opportunities.

**The design.** At each pre-specified eligible decision, the harness randomly assigns intervention or control (equal probability in the experiments) and lets the agent continue, in the style of micro-randomized trials. It needs only black-box access to the agent. Three estimands: a blip effect with randomized continuation (cross-fitted AIPW, no outcome-model correctness needed), a blip with no-treatment continuation (structural nested mean models + g-estimation), and the policy gain of a history-dependent intervention policy versus never intervening (SNMM-based off-policy evaluation).

**The evidence.** Benchmarks: tau-bench Retail and ToolSandbox (tool-call review; GPT-5 nano and Claude Haiku 5.5 agents; GPT-6 Luna as reviewer) and ImpossibleBench (adversarial feedback; Qwen3.5 Flash; 1,218 analysed episodes). In Retail with GPT-5 nano, review effects are positive for reads and writes, with intervals above zero for order cancellation and item changes and including zero for payment changes. For Haiku 5.5 the all-reads average is near zero, yet account-detail calls alone are positive, which the aggregate hides. For nine selective policies, SNMM estimates (reusing 2,280 randomized trajectories) were positive in all nine and overlapped the direct Monte Carlo rollouts (456 episodes per policy) for every policy; the direct estimate fell inside the SNMM interval for eight. In ToolSandbox, "Time and calculations" has a blip of +1.8 [-0.2, 3.8] while the timestamp-construction tool alone has +6.9 [1.2, 12.6].

**Limits (per the authors).** Pointwise intervals ignore multiple comparisons; SNMM intervals are valid only if the blip model is well specified; small models on possibly saturated benchmarks; hand-designed history features; interventions only at fixed, well-specified decision points.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** The useful part is the separation of what randomization gives for free (the AIPW blip) from what needs a model (SNMM estimands). Treat the numbers as a method demonstration, not evidence about frontier agents.
