## 🔎 Deep dive: The Window Lever: Longer Observation Windows Outweigh the Observed Effects of Data, Capacity, and Architecture in Customer Behavior Forecasting

*Amazon (arXiv cs.LG, 19 Nov 2026) · forecasting-timeseries (Core DS) · Core-DS pick (rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS, so Core DS was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The question.** For customer-level forecasts, which lever should a team pull first: more data, a bigger model, a newer architecture, or more history per customer? Sowrirajan and Wang forecast each customer's 364-day forward activity, scored by log-space R² on log(1+y). They hold the export, the test anchor weeks, the target and the evaluation fixed, then sweep one lever at a time across eight model families: transformer, GRU, LSTM, time-biased attention, a decay-gated recurrent model, LightGBM, a 6-feature ridge and a one-feature linear model. They internally registered four falsifiable predictions before the evaluations that decided them.

**What they found.**
- **Window.** Going from an 8- to a 16-week window adds **+0.070 to +0.107 log-space R²** in every family at the 1M tier. The gain is similar at 10M (+0.0737 vs +0.0731, 3-seed).
- **Other levers.** Tier 1M→10M moves sequence models by **≤0.006**. Doubling width or depth: **≤0.005**. Five sequence architectures at 16 weeks: within **~0.005**. With a three-seed detection floor of 0.0059, the authors call these "bounded but unresolved", not nulls.
- **Selection bias.** A longer window changes who is scored. On **matched cohorts** (the same customer-weeks at every window) the gain is **+0.096 vs +0.075** raw, so the naive comparison understates it. Longer windows also admit up to 10% more, and colder, training customers, which matching doesn't fix.
- **Saturation by activity.** The pooled curve plateaus at about 22–24 weeks. The heaviest decile reaches half its gain in **~3.9 weeks**, while the lighter half is still climbing at 16 weeks. Past 24 weeks there is little left at any activity level.
- **Model side.** MS-RoPE (rotary encoding pinned to hour/day/week/year bands) adds **+0.021** and stacks nearly additively with the best loss, to 0.6060.

**Limits (stated by the authors).** All test anchors sit in one ~8-week band, so a longer window also reaches back into the holidays. Window length and calendar coverage aren't separated, and an anchor-varying control was voided by its own pre-registered criterion. A ridge probe on holiday-free training anchors keeps about 72% of its gain. The only fully blind registered prediction failed: the model predicted +0.014 from 24→32 weeks, and the probe returned −0.0004. The paper reports a robust positive association, not an identified calendar-independent history effect. Many numbers are single-seed, and the continuous-time baselines had inert clocks in the main runs.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** "More history helps" isn't new to anyone who has built RFM features. The method is what to copy. Matched cohorts remove the selection effect of a longer window. Registered falsifiers get reported even when they fail. Detection floors stop sub-noise effects from being called "no effect". And the calendar confound is stated up front. The advice ("start by lengthening the window, and size history by customer activity") is well supported on this data class. The causal magnitude of history alone is not.
