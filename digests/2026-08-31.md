## Deep dive: From Models to Products — LLMs for Recommendation at Spotify Scale

**Source:** [Spotify Research](https://research.atspotify.com/2026/8/from-models-to-products-llms-for-recommendation-at-spotify-scale) · Category: LLMs & Generative AI · Novelty **4/5**

**Why it's worth your time:** Spotify moved LLM-based recommendation from a
research idea to a live product surface — with a 21-day A/B test at ~20M
impressions per cell to show for it.

### Business context

LLMs are appealing for recommendation because they can reason jointly over
language, user context, and catalog structure, and can in principle be
steered with natural-language instructions instead of retraining. The
practical blocker is **grounding**: ask an LLM to recommend from a huge,
shifting catalog and it will happily hallucinate items that don't exist.
Spotify's goal was to get past that blocker and into production — specifically
to surface podcast episodes listeners wouldn't otherwise find through their
existing habits.

### Technical details

- **NEO**, the general framework, treats recommendation as one case of a
  broader problem: adapting LLMs to reason over language, users, and
  structured domain entities — a template Spotify frames as reusable beyond
  recommendation.
- **GLIDE**, the production system, is built on a **compact 1B-parameter LLM**
  and formulates recommendation as **instruction-following over a discretized
  catalog**, solved via **Semantic IDs** so every output the model generates
  maps to a real catalog item — no hallucinated titles.
- GLIDE conditions on recent listening history and lightweight context, and
  injects **long-term user embeddings as soft prompts** to keep stable
  preferences legible under tight latency budgets.
- Rather than replacing the existing stack, GLIDE plugs in as an **additional
  candidate generator** feeding the existing downstream ranking pipeline — a
  low-risk way to get a still-maturing LLM component into production.

### Impact — potential & realized

- **Realized:** in a 21-day online A/B test on Spotify Home (~20M impressions
  per cell), GLIDE lifted **non-habitual podcast listening by 5.4%** and
  **new-show discovery by 14.3%**, while holding latency and engagement
  guardrails.
- **Potential:** NEO's grounding + steerability recipe is pitched as a
  template for other large-scale discovery/retrieval problems, not just
  recommendation.

### Claude's Take

**Novelty 4/5.** Plenty of research proposes LLM-based recommendation; a live
A/B test with real traffic and a genuine lift on a metric as hard to move as
new-show discovery is comparatively rare. The recipe — semantic IDs for
grounding, a small dedicated LLM, soft-prompted long-term preferences — is
production-pragmatic rather than fundamentally new, hence 4 and not 5.

**Similar work:** Spotify's own semantic-ID generative retrieval for podcasts
and its adaptive-decoding (SPDD) technique (both in this bank); Netflix's
GenRec, a different production LLM-native recommender.

**Jargon:** _grounding_ = constraining generation to real, valid items instead
of free text; _semantic ID_ = a short discrete code sequence standing in for
an item; _soft prompt_ = a learned vector injected into the model's input in
place of natural-language tokens.

## Deep dive: From User Sequences to Scaling Laws — A Multi-Stage Architecture for Meta's Ads Ranking

**Source:** [Engineering at Meta](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/08/05/ml-applications/from-user-sequences-to-scaling-laws-a-multi-stage-architecture-for-metas-ads-ranking/) · Category: Personalization & Recsys · Novelty **4/5**

**Why it's worth your time:** Meta shows that sequence models of user behavior
in ads ranking obey LLM-style power-law scaling — but only once a specific
design choice (semantic features) is present — and then engineers around the
latency constraint that scaling would otherwise blow through.

### Business context

Meta's ranking systems process billions of daily interactions, and the order
and timing of those interactions carry intent signal that flat feature
aggregates lose. LLM scaling laws show scale reliably buys quality in
language modeling; Meta's question was whether the same predictable payoff
holds for sequence models of user behavior in **latency-sensitive ads
ranking**, where every millisecond of inference cost is a hard production
constraint unlike batch LLM training.

### Technical details

- **LLaTTE** (LLM-style Latent Transformers for Temporal Events) is a
  scalable transformer modeling user behavior as sequences of timestamped
  events — views, clicks, conversions — built as a shared backbone for ads
  ranking.
- **Power-law scaling, conditional on semantic features.** Sequence-model
  quality scales as a predictable power law with capacity, similar to LLM
  scaling laws — but only once richer, meaning-carrying **semantic features**
  are included. Without them the curve bends and plateaus earlier.
- **Two-stage architecture for latency.** An **online ranking model** bounded
  by request-time latency, plus an **asynchronous offline user model** doing
  the heavy long-context computation off the critical path. Scaling the
  online model yields steeper per-compute gains; scaling the offline model
  improves more gradually but sidesteps latency limits entirely.
- **Length and diversity both help.** Quality keeps improving as sequences
  get longer, and a diverse mix of action types outperforms sequences built
  from a single action type.

### Impact — potential & realized

- **Realized:** deployed as **the largest user model at Meta**, the
  framework drove a **4.3% conversion uplift** on Facebook Feed and Reels
  with minimal added serving overhead.
- **Potential:** a principled way to forecast the ROI of further scaling
  investment in ranking sequence models, rather than scaling ad hoc.

### Claude's Take

**Novelty 4/5.** Plenty of teams scale up ranking sequence models and report
a lift; fewer characterize the scaling behavior as a power law conditional on
a specific design choice, then engineer an async offline/online split to
actually capture it in production under a hard latency budget. That
combination is a step beyond "we made the model bigger" — though the
LLM-scaling-law framing itself is imported from language modeling, hence 4
rather than 5.

**Similar work:** Meta's own GEM Training (training-efficiency companion,
also in this batch); Netflix's "Towards Generalizable and Efficient
Large-Scale Generative Recommenders" (a close scaling-law parallel).

**Jargon:** _scaling law_ = an empirical power-law relationship between
model/data/compute size and quality; _semantic features_ = feature
representations encoding an event's meaning rather than a raw ID.
