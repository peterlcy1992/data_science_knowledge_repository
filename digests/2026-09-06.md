## Deep dive: JourneyFormer: Encoding Airbnb Guest Journey with Sequence Modeling

**Source:** [Airbnb Tech Blog / arXiv (KDD 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19108) · Category: Personalization & Recommender Systems · Novelty **4/5**

**Why it's worth your time:** it replaces hundreds of hand-crafted "what this
guest likes" statistics with a transformer that reads the raw event history
directly — and it comes with an unusually rich set of production ablations
(what happens if you drop feed-forward layers, drop hierarchical location
IDs, or change sequence length) plus real online A/B numbers across two
separate ranking surfaces.

### Business context

Airbnb's search ranker previously captured guest preference through hundreds
of hand-engineered aggregate features (average price booked, preferred room
type, and so on) computed from a guest's history. As that feature count
grew, the approach became harder to scale and threw away order, recency, and
co-occurrence information a raw event sequence preserves. But guest
histories are hard to model directly: they span years, mix a few
high-value booking events with a flood of low-signal browsing events, and
the label of interest (an eventual booking) is extremely sparse next to
views.

### Technical details

JourneyFormer splits history into two sequences — a **long-term** stream of
non-view events (bookings, reviews, cancellations) from the past 7 years,
capped at 80 events, and a **short-term** stream of views from the past 21
days, capped at 200 events. Location uses all 14 S2Cell hierarchy levels at
once so the model gets coarse and fine-grained geography simultaneously. The
encoder is a 4-layer transformer with 128-dim embeddings, causal
self-attention, and — counterintuitively — **no feed-forward sublayers**,
which the team found actually improved performance in this sparse-label,
long-sequence regime. Output embeddings are downscaled to 32 dimensions
before being written to storage. Training four throughput optimizations
(search batching, sequence bucketization, sparse computation, plus their
combination) delivered roughly a 4x training speedup versus a naive
implementation.

### Impact — potential & realized

- **Realized (offline):** +1.48% NDCG for the combined long+short model over
  the production baseline.
- **Realized (online, 3-week A/B):** search ranking saw +0.55% bookers,
  +0.82% booked nights, +0.90% views (all p<0.01); email ranking saw +0.16%
  bookers, +0.23% booked nights, and a striking +5.04% email views.
- **Potential:** the gains held up for at least 30 days post-training with
  under 0.1% decline, suggesting the representation is robust enough to not
  need an aggressive retraining cadence — useful evidence for anyone weighing
  how often to refresh a similar sequence model in production.

### Claude's Take

**Novelty 4/5.** Transformers over user event sequences aren't new, but the
specific engineering choices are genuinely interesting: dropping feed-forward
layers is not something you'd guess a priori, and the long/short sequence
split with different retention windows is a sensible, concrete answer to
"guest journeys are long and sparse" — a problem a lot of "just throw a
transformer at it" write-ups gloss over.

**Similar work:** LinkedIn's Feed SR (a comparable decoder-only transformer
over user history, applied to feed ranking instead of search); Netflix's
generalizable generative recommenders (a contrasting generative-retrieval
take on large-scale sequence modeling); Airbnb's own prior Learning-to-Rank
for Maps work, which JourneyFormer's user-representation side extends.

**Jargon:** _S2Cell_ = Google's hierarchical geospatial indexing scheme that
divides the Earth into a quadtree of cells at multiple zoom levels;
_causal masking_ = an attention constraint that stops a sequence position
from seeing anything that comes after it, preserving correct time order.
