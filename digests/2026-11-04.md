## 🔎 Deep dive: Statistical Detection of Positional Bias in LLM-Based Recommender Systems: Methodologies and Empirical Evidence

*arXiv cs.IR (Peláez-González, Herrera, Herrera-Poyatos, University of Granada) · personalization-recsys (AI/ML-systems) · AI/ML-systems pick (rotation: 2 of the last 4 deep dives were Core DS, so AI/ML-systems was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** LLM-based conversational recommenders built on RAG retrieve top-k products, place them in a prompt and ask the model to recommend one. The order of candidates in the prompt is an arbitrary choice, yet the recommendation may depend on it.

**The setup.** Real product catalog, title embeddings with cosine-distance retrieval, a fixed prompt template, 2,000 template-generated queries × 20 permutations = 40,000 instances. Models: LLaMA 3.1 (8B, 70B), LLaMA 4 Scout, GPT-OSS (20B, 120B) and three DeepSeek R1 distills. Two tests: POB-ICM (contingency matrix + chi-square independence) and POB-UNBP (permutation-based uniformity null, valid under stochastic decoding). Three metrics: permutation sensitivity (PS), positional bias (PB) and strong positional bias (sPB, where position displaces the pick to clearly less relevant items).

**Findings.**
- Both tests reject positional independence/uniformity for every evaluated model.
- At k=5, PS is 15.5% for GPT-OSS 120B and 34.0% for DeepSeek R1 7B (PB 4.4% and 5.6%).
- PS grows monotonically with k (DeepSeek R1 7B: 34.0% → 58.1% from k=5 to k=20); sPB rises for every model; LLaMA 4 Scout's PB climbs from 2.9% to 33.8%.
- Robustness sweeps over temperature and candidate-set size lead the authors to call the bias structural and model-dependent. They recommend routine auditing and controls such as order randomization.

**Caveats.** Single templated testbed and catalog, single-turn top-1 recommendation; code is public.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** A clean statistics-flavoured audit recipe (reorder, recount, test against a uniformity null) with a useful metric separating harmless ties from relevance displacement. The headline is stronger as a method than as a finding, since position sensitivity of LLMs is well known.
