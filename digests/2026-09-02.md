## Deep dive: Our Early Journey to Transform Instacart's Discovery Recommendations with LLMs

**Source:** [Instacart Tech](https://tech.instacart.com/our-early-journey-to-transform-instacarts-discovery-recommendations-with-llms-cf4591a8602b) · Category: LLMs & Generative AI · Novelty **4/5**

**Why it's worth your time:** it's a rare, honestly-reported early-stage look
at a genuinely new architecture pattern — an LLM generating page *structure*
top-down, rather than either static curation or an LLM picking final products
directly — with a smart, exportable trick for making generative-UI evaluation
affordable at scale.

### Business context

Instacart's Shopping Hub is the discovery page a shopper sees right after
choosing a retailer, and it shapes the entire session that follows. It
historically ran on static, manually curated content libraries, which created
two problems: personalization didn't scale (authoring per-segment placements
was slow and labor-intensive), and the page lacked cohesion, since different
teams built placements independently rather than as one intentional whole.

### Technical details

- **Top-down vs. bottoms-up generation.** The team weighed generating products
  first and clustering them into placements (bottoms-up) against generating
  page structure/themes first and populating them after (top-down), choosing
  **top-down** as the better balance of personalization, cohesion, and
  adaptability given real-time latency limits and catalog turnover.
- **Phase 1 — page design.** An LLM page-design agent generates personalized,
  high-level shopping themes (e.g., a "Breakfast" placement tailored per user)
  from purchase history and engagement signals.
- **Phase 2 — retrieval keyword generation.** Teacher-student fine-tuning plus
  **retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** maps themes to retrieval-compatible
  descriptors; constraining candidates via catalog embeddings alone cut
  generation cost **15–20%**.
- **Phase 3 — quality/diversity filtering.** Embedding-based deduplication,
  **LLM-as-a-judge** scoring (cohesion, diversity, brand alignment,
  theme-product match), and a fine-tuned cross-encoder for relevance.
- **Phase 4 — ranking.** Filtered, cached placements hand off to Instacart's
  existing ranking services for final product selection — the generative
  pipeline doesn't pick final products itself.
- **Evaluation as a first-class problem.** A three-layer approach: the
  LLM-judge suite above, a fine-tuned **DeBERTa** classifier for product-title
  relevance (a claimed **99% cost reduction** versus closed-weight LLM
  inference at the same task), and classical proxy signals (purchase-history
  overlap, predicted engagement, placement density).

### Impact — potential & realized

- **Realized:** a qualitative shift from rigid, single-category placements to
  multi-category, history-aware ones; initial A/B results are described as
  "quite promising," though no specific lift numbers are disclosed — the team
  is explicit this is early-stage, not a completed system overhaul.
- **Potential:** a reusable pattern — LLM-generated page structure, RAG-
  grounded content, existing ranking kept as the final arbiter — for any
  discovery surface still built on static content, plus the cheap-classifier-
  distillation evaluation trick as a template for affordable generative-UI
  evals more broadly.

### Claude's Take

**Novelty 4/5.** Generating page structure top-down with an LLM agent,
constraining content generation with RAG, then handing off to existing
ranking infrastructure is a real architectural departure from both static
curation and "LLM picks products directly" designs. Distilling a cheap
DeBERTa classifier (99% cheaper than LLM inference) for relevance filtering at
scale is a smart, exportable pattern for keeping generative-UI systems
affordable. It lands at 4 rather than 5 because the write-up is upfront that
this is early — no hard lift numbers are given yet.

**Similar work:** Netflix's GenRec (another production LLM-in-the-loop
recommender, there as a ranker over a catalog-aware head rather than a
page-structure generator); Spotify's GLIDE/NEO work (a different grounding
strategy — semantic IDs — versus this system's RAG-constrained keyword
generation); CVS's Word2Vec+LLM YMAL system (a much lighter LLM use — offline
metadata enrichment — versus this system's LLM-driven generation and
multi-stage judge evaluation).

**Jargon:** _Generative UI_ = a user interface generated per-user by a model
at request time rather than assembled from fixed components; _LLM-as-a-judge_
= using an LLM to score generated content quality as a scalable substitute
for human review; _cross-encoder_ = a model scoring a pair of inputs jointly
rather than embedding each separately, more accurate but more expensive than
embedding similarity.
