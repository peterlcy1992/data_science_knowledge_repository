## 🔎 Deep Dive: Why Spotify Is Not Using Bayesian A/B Testing

**Source:** [Spotify Engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/9/why-spotify-is-not-using-bayesian-a-b-testing) · Published 2026-09
**Category:** Experimentation & Causal Inference

### TL;DR

Spotify argues, with derivations rather than vibes, that most of the industry excitement around Bayesian A/B testing rests on claims that either collapse to standard frequentist practice under realistic conditions or require infrastructure investments — well-calibrated empirical priors, Bayes-factor stopping rules, large clean historical archives — that most organizations, including Spotify, can't actually sustain. Their conclusion: don't add a second experimentation framework without a proven, distinct benefit.

### Business context

Experimentation platforms face constant pressure to add a Bayesian mode alongside frequentist testing, driven by marketing claims about more intuitive probability statements, "free" continuous peeking, automatic multiple-metric handling, and winner's-curse protection. Spotify's program prioritizes minimizing harmful shipped changes and keeping results trustworthy and interpretable across many teams — and supporting two parallel inference paradigms means real organizational cost (different planning, monitoring, and output formats). Before paying that cost, Spotify checked whether the claimed Bayesian advantages actually hold up.

### Technical details

The central mathematical point: under a flat prior and the standard two-group normal model — the default most platforms actually ship — Bayesian and frequentist procedures produce numerically identical outputs; the posterior mean equals the maximum-likelihood estimate, and posterior-probability thresholds map directly onto frequentist rejection regions.

The team works through four commonly cited Bayesian advantages and shows each requires more than marketing suggests: **continuous peeking** is valid under the Likelihood Principle regardless of stopping rule, but that alone gives no false-positive-rate control — real protection needs Bayes-factor stopping, which Spotify notes no platform actually offers; **automatic multiple-metric handling** requires well-calibrated empirical Bayes priors, Bayes-factor stopping, and 200+ historical experiments to calibrate against, and poor calibration can underperform standard group sequential testing (GST); **winner's-curse shrinkage** only works with an informative prior, and a default flat prior replicates the frequentist bias exactly; and **decision-theoretic optimality** under natural cost functions reduces to a Bayes-factor threshold, converging with rather than diverging from frequentist formulations. A simulation testing the best possible case — an oracle historical prior — still found no power advantage over GST.

### Impact

**Realized:** Spotify is not building a Bayesian A/B testing mode, concluding the claimed benefits either don't materialize under realistic conditions or require calibration infrastructure not currently justified by a proven, distinct payoff.

**Potential:** the broader lesson — that statistical framework choice matters far less than getting experiment design right (sampling, stopping rules, decision procedures) — is transferable to any experimentation platform team facing the same "should we add Bayesian?" question.
