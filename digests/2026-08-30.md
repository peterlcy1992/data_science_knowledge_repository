## Deep dive: MAPS — Multimedia Asset Personalization via Multimodal Embeddings at Netflix

**Source:** [arXiv 2608.18322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18322) · Netflix Tech Blog · Category: Computer Vision & Multimodal · Novelty **4/5**

**Why it's worth your time:** Netflix retired a whole family of bespoke artwork
models by moving to shared multimodal embeddings, and — the real lesson — built an
Embedding Store so future foundation models plug in without re-wiring downstream
systems.

### Business context

The image shown for a title is one of Netflix's highest-leverage personalization
surfaces. Each artwork placement used to be served by its own separately trained
model: expensive to maintain and weak at **cold-start**, exactly when new titles
launch. Netflix wanted one approach that generalizes across surfaces, handles new
assets gracefully, and lets teams reuse foundation models without re-plumbing.

### Technical details

- A **two-tower** recommender augmented with **CLIP image embeddings**; because
  CLIP shares an image–text space, one representation does several jobs.
- A single CLIP-augmented model serves **all five artwork canvas types**,
  replacing five per-canvas models and improving cold-start.
- A lightweight extension makes artwork **query-aware in search** by reusing
  CLIP's joint text–image space.
- **MediaFM**, an in-house multimodal foundation model fusing **visual, audio, and
  timed-text** signals, trained on shots from the catalog.
- The keystone: foundation-model outputs are registered in a central **Embedding
  Store**, so a new/upgraded model integrates **without changing downstream
  training or serving**.

### Impact — potential & realized

- **Realized:** five models collapsed into one; substantial cold-start gains;
  query-aware artwork in search; much less model sprawl.
- **Potential:** "adopt a new foundation model" becomes a registration step rather
  than a cross-team re-integration project — the transferable lesson Netflix
  frames for other practitioners.

### Claude's Take

**Novelty 4/5.** The ingredients (two-tower, CLIP) are old; the contribution is
the systems framing — one shared representation retiring a model family, and the
Embedding Store as a decoupling layer that makes foundation models swappable
dependencies. That's what other large recsys orgs will copy. Not a 5 because it's
an integration advance, not a new modeling result.

**Similar work:** GenRec (LLM-native ranker, in this bank), Spotify's semantic-ID
generative retrieval (in this bank), and Netflix's "Scaling Media ML" pipeline.

**Jargon:** _two-tower_ = separate user and item encoders scored by dot product;
_CLIP_ = image+text in one vector space; _cold-start_ = little/no history yet;
_Embedding Store_ = central service of precomputed vectors many systems read.
