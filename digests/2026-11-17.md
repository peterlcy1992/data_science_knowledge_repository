## 🔎 Deep dive: Label-Free Detection of Structural Bias in LLM-Based Recommender Systems via Randomized Paraphrasing: The Case of Verbosity

*University of Granada / DaSCI (arXiv cs.IR) · personalization-recsys (AI/ML-systems) · AI/ML pick (rotation: 2 of the last 4 deep dives were Core DS, so AI/ML-systems was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** LLMs increasingly act as the recommender in shopping assistants: given a query and *k* retrieved products described in text, pick one. That choice should depend on relevance, but it might also respond to surface features, such as how long a seller's description is. Teams swap the model, edit the prompt or ingest new catalog text all the time, and each change can quietly shift what gets recommended. A standard offline eval would need relevance labels for every query–product pair, which live catalogs rarely have. And if longer listings win, sellers learn to write for the recommender rather than for customers.

**The method: randomization, moved from users to inputs.**
- **Design-based guarantee.** Each retrieved product is rewritten by a paraphrasing model chosen by a random permutation, and candidates are shown in random order. If the recommender ignores wording (and the paraphrasers preserve meaning), every paraphraser must be picked with probability 1/*k*. That gives a valid test with no relevance labels. The converse doesn't hold: biases that cancel out can still look uniform, so uniformity is necessary but not sufficient.
- **Four steps.** (1) A χ² test of uniform selection. (2) A conditional-logit model over each query's candidates, splitting the choice into the unique contributions of length and of relevance/semantic proxies: original–paraphrase embedding similarity, query–description similarity (bge-small-en-v1.5) and a cross-encoder relevance score (ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2). (3) The direction and size of each effect. (4) Cases where length and relevance conflict versus agree.
- **Setup.** 20,000 queries over 50,000 Amazon ML Challenge 2023 products, top-5 retrieval. The paraphrasers are DeepSeek-R1 7B, GPT-OSS 20B, Gemma-3 12B, Falcon-3 7B and the identity (the original text). Nine recommenders run at temperature 0: DeepSeek-R1 7B/8B, Falcon-3 7B/10B, GPT-OSS 20B/120B, Gemma-3 12B/27B and Llama-3.1 8B.

**The evidence.**
- **Seven of nine** recommenders depart significantly from uniform selection. The two GPT-OSS models are the exceptions. Deviations reach ±1.3 pp for DeepSeek-R1 7B and Falcon-3 7B. The pattern doesn't fit a self-preference story: the DeepSeek-R1 recommenders actually avoid the DeepSeek-R1 paraphraser.
- **Length matters in all nine** after controlling for the measured relevance signals. The controlled length coefficient ranges from +0.117 (GPT-OSS 120B, ≈12% higher odds per SD of length) to +0.322 (DeepSeek-R1 7B, ≈38%). Unique R² for length runs from 0.28% to 2.25%.
- **Relevance still dominates.** It has the largest coefficients and a solo R² of 5.99–8.72%. When length and relevance conflict, the more relevant description still wins, but on average by less than half the margin seen when the two agree. The GPT-OSS models are the least length-sensitive and the DeepSeek-R1 distillations the most.
- **Cost and power.** *N* queries cost *N* recommender calls, and the paraphrases are reused across models and prompts. About 110–900 queries give 80% power on the length effect; detecting global non-uniformity takes about 8k–19k queries. The authors call these figures indicative.

**Limits (the paper's).** The "relevance controls" are small proxy models, so the length effect is measured beyond the *measured* relevance signals, not beyond true relevance. A non-significant uniformity test doesn't prove the absence of bias.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** This is an A/A test pointed at the model instead of the users. Randomly assigning who rewrites what gives a design-based null without labels, which makes it as much a data-science contribution as a recsys one. It plugs directly into any LLM-as-ranker or LLM-as-judge pipeline as a regression test on every model or prompt change. The effects are modest in R² terms but consistent across families, and they matter most in marketplaces where sellers control the text.
