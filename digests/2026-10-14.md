**Business context.** Uber's product development runs through structured checkpoints where PRDs circulate across design, engineering, legal, ops, science and leadership. Drafts often arrive missing context (unsupported assumptions, adjacent-system impacts, prior experiments), so reviewers spend time on basic discovery instead of trade-offs. The PRD Evaluator is a fast first-pass reviewer upstream of that process.

**How it works.** (1) Assemble a knowledge base from linked docs, decks, meeting notes, prior experiments and preloaded Uber context (principles, metric definitions, jobs to be done). (2) Classify the proposal to set review depth, from light (UX parity) to full-with-specialised-scrutiny (policy, pricing, marketplace). (3) Score dimensions including Opportunity and Hypothesis, Product Scope, UX and Impact, and Metric and Data Rigor. (4) Emit a scorecard: Ready / Ready with Caveats / Not Ready, a "start here" pointer, and per-gap findings with replacement text and evidence. The post does not name the model or give prompts/architecture.

**Impact.** Qualitative only: dozens of PMs used it early; reviews became sharper and faster. Stated lessons: framework-driven critique beats generic feedback, context matters as much as writing, hard boundaries on critical gaps prevent overstated readiness, prioritise findings, and keep humans as approvers.

**Claude's Take.** Having metric rigor as a first-class review dimension is the DS-relevant idea. There is no validation reported (agreement with human reviewers, false-positive rates), so "sharper discussions" is anecdotal. Expect analogous reviewers for experiment-design docs.
