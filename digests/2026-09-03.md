## Deep dive: Introducing WeatherNext 3, Our Most Advanced and Accurate Global Weather AI Model

**Source:** [Google (DeepMind / Google Research)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/introducing-weathernext-3/) · Category: Forecasting & Time Series · Novelty **4/5**

**Why it's worth your time:** it's a real cadence-and-resolution step-change
for AI weather forecasting — moving from a 6-hour forecast cycle to hourly
updates by ingesting live geostationary satellite data — from the team that
established learned global weather forecasting as a serious alternative to
numerical weather prediction in the first place.

### Business context

Weather forecasting underpins renewable-energy planning, agriculture,
aviation, and emergency response, but prior model generations — including
WeatherNext 2 — only produced new forecasts every 6 hours, using data
available at that fixed cadence. That leaves a real gap between fast-moving
weather and how often a model can update its picture of the world.
WeatherNext 3 closes that gap by ingesting satellite data as soon as it
arrives and issuing an updated global forecast every hour.

### Technical details

- **Single-architecture Functional Generative Network (FGN):** a
  mesh-transformer that unifies the modeling approach across variables and
  lead times, rather than stitching together separate specialized models.
- **Live satellite ingestion:** 1-hour geostationary satellite mosaics plus
  historical analysis data, enabling hourly refresh instead of 6-hourly.
- **Multi-format output:** dense gridded fields, discrete cyclone tracks, and
  sparse station-level forecasts from one model.
- **Resolution:** 5km (surface temperature/moisture), 10km (other surface
  variables), 25km (atmospheric/wind) — roughly **5x sharper** than
  WeatherNext 2.
- **Training data:** NASA IMERG precipitation data, satellite-radar-based
  global precipitation reanalysis, and sparse weather-station observations.
- **Distribution:** feeds Google Search, the Gemini app, Google Maps and the
  Maps Platform Weather API, and Earth Engine, plus direct BigQuery/GCS access.

### Impact — potential & realized

- **Realized:** up to **60%** more accurate precipitation forecasts vs.
  IMERG, **30%** vs. MRMS radar estimates, **10%** vs. rain-gauge
  observations at early lead times; up to **50%** more accurate day-ahead
  precipitation forecasts; refresh cadence improved from 6 hours to hourly.
- **Potential:** a template for near-real-time environmental monitoring more
  broadly — renewable-energy siting/dispatch, agricultural planning around
  short-notice shifts, faster emergency-response lead times.

### Claude's Take

**Novelty 4/5.** Feeding live satellite data into a weather foundation model
to shrink the forecast-update interval is a genuine advance over the
6-hour-cycle norm most weather AI (and numerical weather prediction) still
uses — the kind of improvement that changes what a forecast is useful for,
not just how accurate a fixed-cadence forecast is. It lands at 4 rather than
5 because AI weather foundation models (GraphCast, WeatherNext 2) already
established the core paradigm; this is Google's own next iteration rather
than a new one, and no parameter counts or training compute are disclosed.

**Similar work:** TimesFM-3 (a sibling Google forecasting foundation model
from the same week, general-purpose and zero-shot rather than
weather-specific — also covered below); GraphCast (the earlier DeepMind model
that established graph-neural-network-based global forecasting).

**Jargon:** _Functional Generative Network_ = a generative architecture that
outputs a distribution over possible future states rather than one
deterministic forecast; _geostationary satellite mosaic_ = an image stitched
from satellites fixed relative to a point on Earth, giving continuous
regional coverage; _IMERG_ = NASA's satellite-based global precipitation
estimate dataset, used here as both training data and accuracy benchmark.
