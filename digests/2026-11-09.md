## 🔎 Deep dive: When Predictive Fit Misleads: Selecting Marketing Mix Models Using Experimental RoAS Benchmarks

*arXiv stat.AP (Hao Chen, Alvin Lim et al., Measured Inc.) · product-analytics (Core DS) · Core DS pick (rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS, so Core DS was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** Marketing Mix Models (MMMs) estimate channel-level return on ad spend (RoAS) to guide budget allocation. Teams usually choose between candidate specifications by out-of-sample R², MAPE or RMSE, and common workflows (Robyn, Meridian) include predictive diagnostics. The paper's narrow claim: when the target is channel-level causal RoAS, predictive fit alone is not a sufficient selection criterion.

**The setup.** Motivating simulation: 5 locations, 208 weeks, 4 channels. A correctly specified model (adstock + saturation) versus a misspecified linear-with-trend model. The misspecified one fits better out of sample (R² 0.93 vs 0.90; MAPE 9.07% vs 10.38%) but recovers RoAS worse (RoAS MAE 4.40 vs 3.12), overstating all four channels by +172% to +241% because its trend term absorbs long-term growth and credits it to concurrent spend. The proposed workflow fits candidate saturation/adstock specifications by constrained maximum likelihood, computes RoAS from counterfactual predictions with a channel's spend set to zero, and selects the specification that minimizes RoAS error against benchmark RoAS (known in simulation, experimental in applications). Without benchmarks it suggests reporting RoAS across plausible specifications plus a descriptive consensus diagnostic.

**The evidence.** Across 320 simulated datasets per condition where candidates differ only in saturation form, the highest-R² model was the RoAS-best model in 17.5% of cases (95% CI 13.7–22.0%), below the 25% expected from a random pick among four; Kendall correlation between R² ranking and RoAS-accuracy ranking was −0.02; the R²-selected model had on average 1.47× the RoAS error of the best one. When every candidate omits adstock, agreement rises to 55.3% (penalty 1.16×), so fit is more informative under severe shared misspecification. Benchmarks with up to 50% noise still picked more accurate models than R². In a real application (156 weeks, 3 digital channels, experimental RoAS benchmarks), two models with nearly identical R² differed by 60% in RoAS accuracy.

**Limits (per the authors).** Results are specific to the simulated DGPs, candidate sets and spend processes; all candidates use geometric carryover; the RoAS is an average incremental return over the observed spend path, not marginal RoAS; code and real data are available only on request. Authors work at an MMM vendor.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** Not a new estimator, but replicated evidence that "best forecast" and "best causal estimate" are different selection problems, with a directly actionable workflow: treat geo-experiment results as calibration targets for model selection, not just a final check.
