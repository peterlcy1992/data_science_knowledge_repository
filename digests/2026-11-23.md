## 🔎 Deep dive: Combining Past Experimental Data for Efficient Estimation: A Shrinkage Approach

*Zhang, Ham & Jasin, University of Michigan Ross (arXiv stat.ME, 20 Nov 2026) · experimentation-causal (Core DS) · Core-DS pick (rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS, so Core DS was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The question.** Mature experimentation programs keep big archives of past A/B tests. The authors note these are "typically used only qualitatively". Can past experiments on *related but different* interventions lower the mean squared error of one new treatment-effect estimate? Their biases relative to the new target are unknown in size and sign. The paper targets point-estimate accuracy (MSE), which it distinguishes from tighter CIs or better tests.

**What they show.**
- **Oracle.** Blend the new estimate Ā with archive means B̄ᵢ: θ̂ = (1 − Σwᵢ)Ā + Σwᵢ·B̄ᵢ. With one auxiliary study, the optimal weight is w* = V_A/(ε² + V_A + V_B). This **strictly lowers MSE for any finite bias**. With M studies, w* = V_A·Q⁻¹·1. The estimand is a single number, so James–Stein dominance results don't apply.
- **General plug-in.** Sample splitting: a pilot split estimates each study's bias and variance to set the weights, and a separate split forms the estimate. As M grows, its MSE depends on the archive's **average bias *e*** and its **bias dispersion δ**. The effect of δ is non-monotone: dispersed biases give room to cancel bias, but the cancellation targets *estimated* bias, so it overfits.
- **Pooled plug-in.** It restricts study-specific reweighting, so relative weights follow estimated precision only. That limits the overfitting.
- **FH (fixed weights + test gate).** Pool everything with sample-size weights, but only if |archive mean − Ā| passes a t-test at level α. Otherwise use Ā alone. As bias grows its MSE falls back to V_A, but it is always strictly above min{e², V_A}, so it gives up some gains when the archive is good.
- **Decision table.** Small *e* and small δ → general plug-in. Small *e* and large δ → pooled plug-in. Large *e* → FH.

**Evidence.** Simulation only, with no real archive. Gaussian data, θ = 10, V_A = 0.64, archives of up to M = 80 studies, 50,000 replications, and four (e, δ) settings: (0.15, 0.05), (0.15, 12), (5, 2) and (5, 36). The rankings above hold in the simulations. Both plug-ins are "substantially" worse than no borrowing when *e* is large, while FH stays near V_A. Convergence to the large-M limits is slower and noisier when δ is large. The authors argue big platforms sit in the favourable quadrant, citing Kohavi et al.: 80–90% of Google/Bing experiments have near-zero effects. They also give large-M confidence intervals.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** The building blocks are familiar: Stein-type shrinkage, Rosenman et al.'s experimental/observational blending, and pretest estimators. The contribution is the single-estimand framing plus a two-number diagnosis that gives a usable decision table. The finding that bias dispersion cuts both ways is genuinely non-obvious. Before adopting it, note that the evidence is simulation only. How you define "related past experiments" effectively sets *e*. And an MSE-optimal point estimate shrunk toward the archive is not a ship/no-ship test, which the paper says itself. It is a natural frequentist counterpart to the Bayesian priors some A/B platforms already use.
