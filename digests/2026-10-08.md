## 🔬 Deep Dive: LLM-based Relevance Assessment for Web-Scale Search Evaluation at Pinterest

**Source:** [arXiv / Pinterest Search (Wang, Whitworth, Cheung, Zhang, Kamath)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03764) · Published 2025-09 · Category: Search & Ranking (AI/ML, measurement-flavoured)

*(AI/ML-systems was due in the rotation: the last 4 deep dives were 2 Core DS, 2 AI/ML.)*

**Business.** Search relevance in A/B tests is normally judged by human annotators: slow, costly, and limited to small query samples, which means large minimum detectable effects (MDE).

**Technical.** A fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa-large cross-encoder (chosen over mBERT, T5, mDeBERTa and Llama-3-8B, which was slightly better but ~6x costlier) was trained on ~2.6M human-labelled query-Pin pairs on a 5-point scale, using text-only features (Pin text, BLIP captions, linked page and board text, engaged queries). It matches humans exactly 73.7% and within one level 91.7% (US; Kendall tau 0.652). Cheap labels enable better sampling: paired control/treatment queries, strata by interest x popularity, Neyman optimal allocation, metric sDCG@25.

**Impact.** Authors report MDE falling from ~1.3–1.5% to ≤0.25%, with stratification cutting standard deviation 52% (n=2000) to 67% (n=5000). Weaker in France/Germany (tau ~0.47), text-only, and humans are still used when the experiment changes the relevance model itself.

**Claude's Take (3/5).** The judge is a standard fine-tuned encoder; the DS lesson is that cheap labels let you redesign the sampling, and that is where most of the MDE gain comes from.
