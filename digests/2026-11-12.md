## 🔎 Deep dive: Generative Search and Recommendation at Shopify

*arXiv cs.IR (Shopify: Kangaspunta, Hajji, Fofadiya et al.) · personalization-recsys (AI/ML, tied to DS measurement and online A/B tests) · AI/ML pick (rotation: the last 4 deep dives included 2 Core DS, so AI/ML-systems was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** Shopify runs search (Catalog API, merchant storefronts, the Shop app) and recommendations (Shop app home feed, ads) over a catalog of billions of products. Which products are eligible depends on context such as the buyer's market, and retrieval has to finish within tens of milliseconds. The existing stack is a typical industrial mix: lexical, dense and two-tower retrievers, plus a separate HSTU-style generative recommender over item IDs. The paper's bet is that one LLM that generates product IDs derived from product content can give search and recommendation a shared representation and training objective.

**The idea.** *SID-LLM* represents each product by a three-codeword semantic ID. The IDs come from RQ-KMeans quantization of a 768-dim search embedding, with codebooks fit on the top 25M products by GMV. The authors chose an 8192-8192-8192 codebook over a better-scoring 32768-first-level one to keep p95-latency headroom. They compared LFM2-2.6B with Qwen3 models from 1.7B to 14B. Qwen3 gave better average quality at higher latency, so the lowest-latency LFM2-2.6B is deployed. The two surfaces are served differently:
- **Search:** multi-token prediction (MTP) emits all three codewords in one pass. That roughly halves median latency but scores below autoregressive decoding. A lightweight ranker distilled from a Qwen3.5-9B relevance judge re-scores MTP's 250 candidates and more than recovers the gap.
- **Recommendations:** an autoregressive model with a parameter-free decode turns each ID back into products. Each product is scored by log p(ID | user) plus the log of its popularity share within the ID's cluster. This raised Recall@50 by 15.8% over taking one product per ID. 78.9% of predicted IDs map to more than one product.

Distilling task experts into one unified search+recs student improved recommendation Recall@50 by 1.76% (discovery Recall@50 by 10.03%), with no statistically detected loss in search quality. The unified student is still experimental.

**How they measure (the DS angle).**
- *OneDCG@10* combines an LLM judge's relevance grade with a static quality score. It is normalized by a constant, not by each query's ideal DCG, so scores are comparable across queries.
- The judge, query set, index and inference code all changed during the project. Each table therefore names a fixed evaluation protocol, and absolute scores are compared only within a table.
- *Incremental pool recall* measures a retriever's contribution: remove one generator from the union of all generators' top-200 candidates and see how much recall of next-week orders falls.

**The evidence.**
- **Search-stack ablation:** removing SID-LLM costs 6.5% OneDCG@10 (7.7% on personalized search). SID-LLM alone is only 12.8% below the whole stack (5.9% on personalized search).
- **Recommendation candidate pool** (70,747 users with a purchase in the held-out week): SID-LLM's Recall@50 is 24.61%, against 17.28% for recent-engagement and 17.21% for HSTU. Its incremental pool recall is 2.65 points, 61% more than the next-best generator.
- **Home-feed A/B test:** attributed orders +7.95%, converting users +8.60% and conversion rate +9.85% on the impacted surface, all significant after Dunnett correction.
- **Search A/B tests:** these "supported deploying" SID-LLM, but the paper gives no lift numbers.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** Semantic-ID generative retrieval itself isn't new; Spotify, WeChat and Tmall versions are already in the bank. What's worth copying is the measurement:
- Leave-one-generator-out incremental pool recall is a better question than standalone recall for any multi-retriever system.
- Naming a fixed evaluation protocol is good hygiene when the LLM judge keeps changing.

Caveats:
- OneDCG folds a popularity/brand score into "relevance", so it partly rewards popularity.
- The search online lifts aren't quantified.
- The home-feed lift covers the impacted surface only.
- The unified model isn't in production.
