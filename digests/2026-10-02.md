## 🔬 Deep Dive: Accelerating A/B-Tests with Counterfactual Estimation: Reducing Variance through Policy Overlap

**Source:** [arXiv (Olivier Jeunen)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14604) · Published 2026-07 · Category: Experimentation & Causal

*(Core DS was due in the rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS.)*

**Business.** Recommender, retrieval and LLM-interface experiments often compare two policies that agree on most decisions, yet difference-in-means pays the full outcome variance, making tests slower than necessary.

**Technical.** Randomised assignment is framed as a meta-policy over actions; Δ-off-policy estimators (IPW and doubly robust variants) give an unbiased estimator whose variance scales with policy divergence rather than raw outcome variance. Stated limits: weak overlap, propensity-estimation bias–variance trade-offs, degradation when policies diverge a lot.

**Impact.** Variance reductions reported on synthetic and semi-synthetic benchmarks (roughly 40–70% depending on overlap per the summary reviewed — approximate, unverified). No production deployment reported.

**Claude's Take (4/5).** Exploits structure in the treatment itself rather than pre-period data, so it could stack with CUPED. It needs both policies' action probabilities — realistic for ranking, harder for black-box LLM systems.
