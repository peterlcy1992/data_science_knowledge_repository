## 🔎 Deep dive: Conformal Policy Selection: When to Deploy AI Decisions for Targeted Intervention

*arXiv stat.ME (Cao, Gao, Saar-Tsechansky & Yin), Oct 2026 · experimentation-causal · novelty 4/5*
Why this pick: Core DS was due (only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS), and this is a fresh, solid Core-DS entry.

🎙️ Deep-dive podcast: podcasts/2026-10-19-arxiv-conformal-policy-selection-fdr-deployment.m4a
🎙️ Episode S1E41 — cover ✓, RSS draft ✓ (unpublished; publish from the RSS.com dashboard)

**Business context.** Targeted interventions (treatments, offers, outreach) are usually approved on average uplift, which can hide a subpopulation where a globally trained policy generalizes badly and does harm. The paper frames deployment as a per-individual safety question: for which units can we be confident the outcome under the AI policy exceeds a user-specified threshold?

**How it works.** For each candidate unit the method tests whether the outcome under the target (AI) policy exceeds a threshold. It builds weighted conformal p-values that correct for distribution shift from the behavior policy that generated the data to the target policy, then applies a two-step selection-and-pruning procedure to control FDR over the selected set. The authors prove finite-sample FDR control and a doubly robust asymptotic guarantee: FDR is controlled if either the outcome model or the propensity model is correctly specified. Detailed algorithm steps were not read (abstract-level).

**Impact (as reported).** Realized (as reported in the abstract): synthetic experiments confirm the guarantees across four misspecification regimes; a semi-synthetic study on real electronic-health-record covariates shows FDR control acting as a deployment safety gate that stops a globally trained policy from giving harmful treatments to a high-risk subpopulation where it generalizes poorly. No numeric results were available to me. Potential: a statistically principled go/no-go gate for personalized offers, notifications or clinical-style interventions.

**Claude's Take.** Treating per-unit deployment as multiple testing with FDR control is a neat reframing: it turns 'is the model good on average?' into a calibrated list of units where it is safe to act. Weighted conformal under policy shift plus a doubly robust guarantee is a real combination, though conformal and FDR ideas are individually established. Caveat: abstract-level read; practical power (how many units survive selection) is unknown.
