## 🔎 Deep dive: Making Experiment Results Comparable through Sitewide Impact Estimation

*Roblox (arXiv stat.AP) · experimentation-causal (Core DS) · Core-DS pick (rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS, so Core DS was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** Roblox runs experiments on very different surfaces: discovery, the virtual economy, notifications, creator tools. They differ in **reach** (how many users a treatment touches) and in how much those users **contribute** to a platform metric. A 5% lift on a niche surface and a 0.5% lift on the home page are not on the same scale. Leadership still has to rank them against each other, and to say how much of year-over-year growth came from shipped products.

**The estimand.** *Sitewide impact* is the relative change in a platform aggregate (e.g. total play time) if the treatment reached 100% of its target population, with everyone else left untreated. Under SUTVA, and assuming the treatment doesn't change reach:
- **Sitewide lift = experiment lift × coverage factor C.** C is the targeted users' share of the sitewide metric when no one is treated. For example, a 2% lift in a population holding 10% of the metric is a 0.2% sitewide lift.
- **Baseline reconstruction.** The no-treatment sitewide mean is estimated by subtracting the in-experiment treatment effect from the observed mean.
- **Not transportability.** The arms are already a random sample of the population a rollout would treat, so there is no covariate shift to correct. Positivity for users outside the target isn't needed, because no effect is carried over to them. The coverage factor plays the same role as the classic *diluted treatment effect* from trigger analysis.

**The parts that go beyond the textbook.**
- **Variant-level ratio metrics** (total crashes ÷ total play hours). Neither the numerator's nor the denominator's coverage factor gives the right answer. So the estimator reconstructs each total separately, adds each component's effect, and then takes the ratio. With one factor, the median error is 3.5% of the sitewide lift, and 29%+ for one experiment in ten.
- **Segments.** Recombine segment *totals*, not averages of segment lifts. Across 155 experiments this cut the mean absolute error versus the reported overall lift from 1.33 to 0.47 pp for time spent, and from 7.01 to 1.68 pp for bookings.
- **Intervals.** The experiment's interval is scaled by C, treating C as known. The authors say plainly that this is slightly too narrow. Daily holdout reads use an O'Brien–Fleming-style band.

**The evidence.**
- **Lift is a poor proxy for impact.** Across 139 experiments significant on time spent, the Spearman correlation between |experiment lift| and |sitewide impact| is **0.20**. Only 5 of the top-10 lifts are in the top-10 impacts.
- **A/A check.** Pre-periods of 1,349 experiments give a median sitewide lift of 0.00004% (signed-rank p = 0.59).
- **Payments holdout** (Jan–Mar 2026, eight innovations). Summed impacts land inside the holdout's interval: 0.41% vs 0.50% on bookings, and 0.22% vs 0.21% on probability of spending Robux. The summed impacts are an upper bound, because the platform doesn't record which arm shipped.
- **Uses.** The method is in production for cross-domain prioritization and for attributing year-over-year growth to launches.

**Limits (the paper's).** SUTVA and fixed reach are both strong and untestable from the experiment alone. Holding reach fixed biases the estimate whenever a treatment changes reach, e.g. downward for a better feature that draws more users to its surface. Computation is costly, and fixed lookback windows fit long-running experiments poorly.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** The identity is old (diluted effects), but the production package is not: baseline reconstruction, a correct ratio-metric estimator with the naive error quantified, segment recombination, and validation against both A/A data and a real holdout. The 0.20 rank correlation is the number to show any planning meeting that ranks bets by "% lift." My main caveat is fixed reach. The features you most want to find, the ones that grow their own surface, are exactly the ones this method underrates.
