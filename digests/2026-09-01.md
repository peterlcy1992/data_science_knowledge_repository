## Deep dive: An Industrial-Scale Sequential Recommender for LinkedIn Feed Ranking (Feed SR)

**Source:** [LinkedIn / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12354) · Category: Search & Ranking · Novelty **5/5**

**Why it's worth your time:** LinkedIn's own paper is unusually candid about
what *didn't* work — including a fine-tuned LLM ranker that never beat the
production baseline — while backing its winning design with real production
A/B numbers and serious GPU-serving engineering.

### Business context

LinkedIn Feed serves over 1.2 billion members. The legacy production
ranker, **DCNv2** (a feature-cross architecture), has no native way to model
a member's long interaction history as an ordered sequence — it works from
aggregated feature crosses instead. LinkedIn built **Feed SR** to test
whether a transformer-based sequential ranker could do better, evaluating it
against both the DCNv2 incumbent and LLM-based ranking alternatives, all
under real production latency and cost constraints.

### Technical details

- **Architecture:** a decoder-only transformer with pre-LayerNorm, **RoPE**
  positional embeddings, and scaled "RescaleAndAdd" residual connections
  (without pre-LN, AUC collapses to 0.5). It processes up to **1,000
  historical impressions** per member.
- **Scoring head:** a "parallel DCNv2" head after the transformer beat
  linear, MLP, stacked-DCNv2, and MMoE alternatives in ablations.
- **Feature engineering:** roughly **80% feature reduction** versus the
  legacy ranker; item-popularity and viewer-author-affinity signals mattered
  most.
- **Serving:** a disaggregated CPU (feature fetch) / GPU (inference) stack
  with Arrow columnar member histories; "shared context batching" scores
  ~512 candidates in one forward pass (an 80x speedup); a custom
  flash-attention CUDA kernel ("SRMIS") gives ~2x speedup over PyTorch's
  built-in SDPA.
- **Rejected alternatives:** an "LLM-Ranker" (fine-tuned LLM ranking via text
  prompts) struggled with numeric features and never beat the production
  baseline online; a TransAct-style history encoder improved offline metrics
  but was too latency-heavy for the existing serving infrastructure.

### Impact — potential & realized

- **Realized:** in a production A/B test against the prior DCNv2 ranker —
  **+2.10% time spent** (DAU +2.38%, WAU +1.84%) and **+3.52%**
  likes/comments/reshares (DAU +4.07%), gains concentrated among active
  members. Feed SR has served the majority of LinkedIn Feed traffic for 3+
  months. Despite being a larger model, it uses roughly **0.2x the training
  energy** and **0.7x the inference energy** of the legacy CPU-served
  pipeline.
- **Potential:** a template combining sequential-transformer ranking with
  serious GPU-serving engineering for teams whose ranking systems are still
  CPU-served feature-cross models.

### Claude's Take

**Novelty 5/5.** This is an unusually numbers-dense industrial paper: real
production A/B results, energy-efficiency numbers, kernel-level engineering
detail, and — notably — honest negative results about approaches that were
tried and didn't ship. That combination of production rigor and candor is
rare in a single write-up.

**Similar work:** NVIDIA's generative-recommenders survey (same industry
shift at the tooling level, including HSTU, which Feed SR explicitly tested
and rejected at matched compute); Netflix's generative-recommender
scaling-law study (a parallel scaling exercise for a similar backbone);
Spotify's LLM-for-recommendation work (another production system that
rejected the literal "just use a big LLM" design).

**Jargon:** _DCNv2_ = a feature-crossing ranking architecture common in
production recommenders before sequence models; _RoPE_ = Rotary Positional
Embedding, encoding token position by rotating embedding vectors; _flash
attention_ = a GPU-memory-efficient algorithm for computing attention
without materializing the full attention matrix.
