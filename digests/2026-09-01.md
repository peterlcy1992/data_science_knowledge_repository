## Deep dive: Sidekick's Continual Learning Loop

**Source:** [Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/sidekicks-continual-learning-loop) · Category: LLMs & Generative AI · Novelty **5/5**

**Why it's worth your time:** Shopify replaced "throw a bigger frontier
model at it" with a closed loop that keeps improving its AI agent's quality
while cutting its serving cost by roughly 96%.

### Business context

Sidekick is Shopify's AI agent. Relying purely on ever-larger frontier-model
prompting to improve its quality gets expensive fast and hits diminishing
returns. Shopify wanted to keep improving Sidekick without perpetually
scaling frontier-model spend, and to do so against a quality signal it could
actually trust — not informal judgment calls about whether a change "felt"
better.

### Technical details

The loop has four stages:

1. **A human-calibrated quality rubric**, validated against human raters
   using **Cohen's kappa** (an inter-rater agreement statistic correcting
   for chance agreement).
2. **Judge calibration** — a **DSPy**-based pipeline tunes an LLM-as-judge
   against that rubric using reflective prompt-optimization techniques
   (**GEPA** and **ACE**), then validates the judge against **production A/B
   test outcomes** before it's trusted as a reward signal.
3. **Prompt/tool "autoresearch"** — automated search over prompt and tool
   configurations.
4. **Continual learning** — production failures are mined for SFT, followed
   by **GRPO** fine-tuning that uses the calibrated judge itself as the
   reward signal.

The system serves a GraphQL agent at **2,000 requests/minute**.

### Impact — potential & realized

- **Realized:** serving cost fell roughly **96%, from ~$27M to ~$1M/year**.
- **Potential:** a template for compounding LLM-agent quality improvements
  without linear cost growth from continually upgrading to bigger frontier
  models — as long as the judge is genuinely validated first.

### Claude's Take

**Novelty 5/5.** LLM-as-judge, DSPy prompt optimization, and GRPO are each
individually known techniques. What stands out is the discipline of
calibrating the judge against human raters *and* real production A/B
outcomes before ever using it as an RL reward — the step that separates
this from plain RLHF against an uncalibrated judge, which tends to reward
gaming the judge rather than genuine improvement. A verifiable, large
production cost number for a real high-traffic agent is rare enough on its
own to merit the top of the scale.

**Similar work:** Shopify's own Gisting (context compression, in this
batch); Uber's software-factory cost-management framework (in this batch,
usage/routing economics instead of quality-driven RL); DoorDash's Flux
cloud-agent platform (in this bank).

**Jargon:** _Cohen's kappa_ = a chance-corrected inter-rater agreement
statistic; _LLM-as-judge_ = using an LLM to score another model's outputs;
_GRPO_ = a reinforcement-learning method that scores a group of sampled
outputs relative to each other rather than needing a separate value
function.
