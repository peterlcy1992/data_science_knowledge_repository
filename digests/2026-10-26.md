## 🔎 Deep dive: Volatility-Corrected Prediction Intervals: Locally Adaptive Uncertainty Quantification for Probabilistic Time-Series Forecasting

*arXiv stat.ME (Cho & Matteson) · forecasting-timeseries · Core DS pick (rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS) · novelty 3/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** Forecast intervals are usually judged by marginal coverage, the share of all observations inside the interval. Forecast errors, though, are serially dependent and heteroskedastic, so an interval can hit its nominal level on average while miscovering systematically in high-volatility stretches. The authors call this *local scale miscalibration*. In their motivating example (Capital Bikeshare daily rentals, rolling 16-step forecasts from Chronos, Moirai 2 and TimesFM 2.5), errors cluster in the high-demand summer months and coverage of the nominal 80% intervals, computed over 100 consecutive forecasts, drops below 60% in some periods.

**The method (VCPI).** Post-hoc, with the forecaster's point forecast untouched. (1) Fit a Gaussian reference scale to the forecaster's reported quantiles. (2) Standardize the residuals by that scale and model the remaining scale correction with a GARCH-family model, e.g. GARCH(1,1): s_t² = ω + a·e²_{t−1} + b·s²_{t−1}. The variance specification and the standardized error distribution are chosen jointly on a temporally ordered validation segment by coverage error, then refitted on the whole calibration window. (3) Multiply the reference scale by the forecast correction and use quantiles of the estimated error distribution to form the interval.

**Theory.** Conditional on a specified shock event and under the paper's assumptions, after a sufficiently large upward volatility jump the first-step GARCH update lands closer to the post-jump oracle interval than the analysed quantile-tracking updates. The reason: quantile tracking reacts to whether the last interval covered, GARCH reacts to how large the miss was.

**Results.** Chronos, Moirai and TimesFM on four datasets (Bike Rental Counts, Colorado River, Electricity Demand, NO2 Concentration), horizons 1–4, 5–8, 9–16, nominal 80%, versus native intervals, ACI, QT, EnbPI and SPCI. The primary metric is rolling RMSCE; VCPI has the smallest RMSCE in the paper's summary, with marginal coverage and width broadly competitive. Example: Bike Rental, horizons 1–4, Chronos RMSCE 0.079 native → 0.051 VCPI (conformal baselines 0.096–0.109). Widths are comparable rather than uniformly smaller (TimesFM bike rental horizons 1–4: 2261 vs 2051 native).

**Limits the authors state.** Parametric GARCH scale model and error distribution (misspecification leaks into intervals); multi-step GARCH forecasts mean-revert quickly, so the correction degrades beyond roughly 4–8 steps; 95% tail coverage is more sensitive to misspecification than moderate levels.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** Local coverage is a known weak spot of conformal intervals and GARCH scaling is classical; the value here is documenting the failure in foundation-model intervals and showing a small post-hoc layer fixes much of it. Read the result as "more stable local coverage at comparable width", not tighter intervals. The rolling-RMSCE check is worth borrowing even without VCPI.
