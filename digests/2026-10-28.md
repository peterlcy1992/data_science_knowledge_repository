## 🔎 Deep dive: LCM-Promo: An LLM-Based Uplift Model for Promotion Allocation

*arXiv cs.LG (Prosus / iFood) · personalization-recsys · AI/ML pick (rotation: 2 of the last 4 deep dives were Core DS, so AI/ML-systems was due; most recently added AI entry with novelty ≥ 3) · novelty 3/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** iFood's production promotion policy, Segment-EMA, forecasts orders per segment-promotion cell with an exponentially weighted moving average. Every new discount, segment or context needs exploration inside its cell, which caps how finely users can be segmented.

**The model.** A 4B-parameter dense model, finetuned on production logs, reads a prompt with session context (day, delivery shift), an LLM-generated user representation and discount options ordered by depth, and predicts one token: the discount at which the user converted (or "no purchase"). Restricting the next-token distribution to valid identifiers and renormalising gives a PMF over a latent conversion threshold; its cumulative sum is a promotion-response curve that is monotone non-decreasing by construction. The curves feed a linear-optimisation allocator under a global budget. Inference runs twice daily on cluster-level sampled curves, about 1 hour a day on 8 H200s with vLLM.

**Results.** One-month production A/B on churn-reactivation users in Brazil (90% Segment-EMA control, 10% LCM-Promo, same budget and allocator): conversion +1%, promotion cost −6% (relative; no intervals reported). Allocation grew from about 50 segments to over 1,000 clusters and from 10 wallet configurations to over a million promotion combinations; it was then rolled out to all churn users. Offline on RCT data with a fixed promotion versus an uplift random forest: AUUC 0.0608 vs 0.0578, Qini 0.0490 vs 0.0466 (LCM-Promo slightly ahead), Uplift@30 0.1626 vs 0.1708 (forest ahead). The authors treat this as validation for online testing, not offline superiority. One lifecycle stage, one platform.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** The LLM is a response-curve estimator whose output is shaped to satisfy the monotonicity an optimiser needs, which is the sensible way to use one in a decision loop. The A/B design is clean, but with no uncertainty on the lifts and a coarse baseline it is hard to separate "LLM" from "finer segmentation".
