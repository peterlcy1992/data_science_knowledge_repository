## 🔬 Deep Dive: Neutral Is Not Free: Evaluating Downside Risk in Neutral Launches

**Source:** [arXiv stat.AP (Alcain, Kang, Garrard, Veits, Nassif, Zaidi)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10223) · Published 2026-10 · Category: Experimentation & Causal Inference (Core DS)

*(Core DS was due in the rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS.)*

**Business.** Many launches, such as migrations, infra upgrades and refactors, are not meant to move metrics; the experiment's job is to show no meaningful harm. Teams often ship if confidence intervals overlap, but absence of evidence is not evidence of safety, especially with thin data.

**Technical.** The paper proposes Expected Bayesian Loss (EBL), a continuous downside-risk measure that captures both how likely a metric is to degrade and how bad the degradation would be. It is computed from standard frequentist estimates (no full Bayesian model), penalises noisy high-variance experiments, and is a tunable guardrail aligned to an organisation's risk appetite. The abstract gives no thresholds or quantitative results.

**Impact.** Realized: the authors claim validation against expert decisions (numbers not in the abstract). Potential: one risk-scaled guardrail number that works across very different sample sizes and makes ship/no-ship criteria auditable. Sourced from the abstract only.

**Claude's Take (4/5).** Expected loss is an old idea; the value is packaging it on top of outputs experimentation platforms already store, so uncertainty is penalised instead of rewarded. Open questions: how the loss scale is calibrated per metric, and how circular the "expert agreement" check is.
