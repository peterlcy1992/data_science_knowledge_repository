## 🔎 Deep dive: Weight Coding: Evolving Web-Scale Recommenders through Direct Weight Edits with LLM Agents

*Hou, Qu, Cheng, Yang, Naikawadi & Subbian, Pinterest (arXiv cs.IR, 22 Nov 2026) · personalization-recsys (AI/ML-systems) · AI/ML pick (rotation: 2 of the last 4 deep dives were Core DS, so AI/ML-systems was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The idea.** Recommenders usually change through retraining or fine-tuning. For this model the base costs 200+ GPU hours and a post-training run about 140, and the paper says "a common launch only improves around 1%". *Weight coding* treats the weights like code instead. An LLM coding agent inspects a production model's structure and parameters, forms hypotheses, and makes direct edits: **rescale, reset, remove, compress, interpolate, steer, search**. Each edit is scored by a cached, forward-only copy of the production evaluator. Engineers set goals in plain language, approve deployment and decide what ships.

**How the loop works.**
- **Scan** primitives on 130 module groups on a dev day. Of 1,258 edits, 47% moved recall@10 by under 0.1 points and 10% gained more than one point.
- **Filter** on other days: 23% of edits that moved dev-day recall by more than a point *reversed sign*, and were dropped.
- **Edit or search.** An evolution strategy (16 antithetic Gaussian pairs per step, centered ranks, Adam) tunes chosen values. Composing edits means searching again with earlier edits in place.
- **Validate.** Held-out days "never guide iteration". Specificity checks look at segments, diversity and popularity. One edit that collapsed embeddings inflated recall through score ties, so ties are now broken pessimistically.

**Offline results (147M-parameter Image Search two-tower retriever, held-out days).**
- Resetting one LayerNorm gain and one offset (128 values): **+10.1 recall@10 points**.
- Searching 2,064 token-mixing values: **+13.7**. Re-searching them with a head edit in place (L07): **+15.0** (+24% relative). Simply *stacking* the two edits had dropped the gain from 14.1 to 10.2.
- Reinitializing the whole 13.6M-parameter interaction module and searching only 2,448 values: **+9.8**.
- Removing all four DHEN branches gains 6.1 points, so the trained branches made "a negative net contribution under this metric".
- Cost: 8.6 GPU hours per loop and four loops, 24.6% of a fine-tune.
- Gains held on a separate corpus, on recall@1000 and on the full corpus, none of which were optimized. Distinct top-10 Pins rose 9–18%.
- Across the funnel: PinRec +2.3/+2.6 pp recall@1 and the 1.37B ranker +0.53% utility NDCG. Many two-tower edits did not transfer.
- One steering coefficient moves fresh Pins' top-10 share from 2% to 70%.

**Online (A/B, ~10M users per arm, about a week, attributed to the edited retrieval source).** Across six all-engagement arms, closeups per impression tracked offline recall gain (Pearson r = 0.97). Save-targeted arms raised **saves per user 8.4–10.7%**, but long clicks fell 15–25% and product share 33–40%. The authors flag that the ranker favours the edited source and cannibalizes other retrievers, so these are not site-wide numbers.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** The ingredients are known: model editing, task arithmetic, checkpoint interpolation, activation steering and ES. The new part is an agent-run engineering loop on a production recommender, ending in an online A/B test, and the size of the effects. A 10-point gain from resetting a few normalization values says a lot about how poorly one training schedule can balance a model. For data scientists the most reusable part is the evaluation design: dev, filter and held-out splits, sign-reversal filtering, a tie-breaking fix after the metric was gamed, and checks on metrics nobody optimized. Read the online lifts with care. They are source-attributed with stated cannibalization, the save arms trade away long clicks and products, and edit durability across checkpoint refreshes is untested.
