## Deep dive — How we built Datadog Experiments

**Source:** [Datadog Engineering Blog](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/how-we-built-datadog-experiments/) · Published 2026-09 · Category: Experimentation & Causal Inference · Novelty 3/5 · Written from the full text

**TL;DR.** After acquiring Eppo, Datadog rebuilt Eppo's experimentation side as Datadog Experiments. The post covers four changes:
- CUPED was reworked from per-variant regression adjustment into "metric augmentation". One set of global coefficients then gives unbiased adjusted results for any segment, and the same approach extends to percentile metrics such as p90.
- Global lift is now defined as Coverage × Local lift, so it can be checked by hand and works for windowed metrics.
- "Copy SQL" exposes the warehouse query behind a number.
- RUM and Product Analytics metrics give an early signal that updates about every 15 minutes.

### 1. Business context

Eppo's feature management became Datadog Feature Flags, and its experimentation became Datadog Experiments. The rebuild's stated goal is to move "from an experiment result to a confident decision more quickly". It works through four levers: statistical efficiency, interpretable impact, verifiability and faster feedback. Each lever answers a pain point in the old product:
- CUPED could not reach segments without refitting a model per segment.
- Global lift and coverage were reported separately and did not combine.
- About 20 pipeline steps sat between raw tables and a reported result, so customers sent their queries to the Eppo team to debug discrepancies.
- Warehouse business metrics such as revenue or retention "can land hours later".

### 2. Technical details

- **Old CUPED.** Eppo fitted a separate subject-level regression model per variant. The lift estimate was unbiased only for the population used to fit those models, so CUPED for a segment needed its own refit.
- **CUPED as metric augmentation.** The post says this is "inspired by CUPED's original authors, who describe the method as a metric augmentation rather than a regression adjustment".
  - Start from the unadjusted lift and add terms that average to zero over many randomizations. They add no bias and can reduce variance.
  - One set of coefficients gives zero-expectation adjustments for *any* segment. So a single model run yields unbiased CUPED-adjusted results for every slice.
  - The post says this "generally reduces variance".
  - Example waterfall: a raw lift of 16.09% becomes a CUPED-adjusted 12.96%, and the estimated run time falls by 51.97%.
- **Percentile CUPED.** Regression CUPED has no obvious subject-level outcome to predict for a percentile. Augmentation simply attaches adjustment terms to the ordinary p90. Datadog claims this "has long been out of reach for commercial experimentation platforms". The post does not say how the percentile terms are built or how their variance is estimated.
- **Global lift = Coverage × Local lift.**
  - Local lift is the relative lift among enrolled subjects.
  - Coverage is the share of the company-wide metric total that the experiment's eligible users account for. It is computed separately from global lift, which is what makes global lift work for windowed metrics.
  - Examples: 50% × 10% = 5%. A product screenshot shows 13.0% × 40.3% = 5.23%.
- **Copy SQL.** Users can copy the query behind a warehouse-native number and run it themselves. Results land "very close" to the dashboard; small gaps come from incremental processing and late data. It reproduces only metrics **without** CUPED adjustment. Diagnostic checks, such as traffic imbalance between variants or a metric with no data, run before results are interpreted.
- **Fast metrics.** RUM and Product Analytics events (pageviews, clicks, errors) update "typically about every 15 minutes", with no separate streaming infrastructure. APM Spans metrics are in Preview. Session Replays of the first exposed users are available within minutes, though confidence intervals are still wide at that stage.

### 3. Impact — potential & realized

- **Realized.** One number: about 52% shorter estimated run time in a single example experiment. The post gives no cross-customer aggregate and no benchmark of percentile CUPED against unadjusted percentiles.
- **Potential.**
  - Segment results stay consistent with the topline.
  - Percentile guardrails (e.g. p90 page-load time) could run on less traffic, so a latency spike "can be caught sooner and rolled back faster".
  - Impact reporting becomes auditable.
  - Debugging moves from vendor support to the customer's own analysts, for non-CUPED metrics.

### Claude's Take *(opinion)*

**Novelty 3/5 — a well-argued platform rebuild. Percentile CUPED via augmentation is the interesting bit, but its details stay off the page.**

The zero-mean augmentation framing is old; the post credits the original CUPED authors. What is new and practical is the consequence: global coefficients give valid adjustment for any segment, and quantiles become adjustable. The percentile claim is the boldest and least documented. An influence-function or linearization construction would be the natural route, but that is my guess, not the post's. Segment gains may also shrink where global coefficients fit a segment poorly. "Unbiased" here should be read as in expectation over randomizations.

The multiplicative global-lift definition is simple and worth copying. Few platforms' "company-wide impact" numbers can be rebuilt from parts. Copy SQL's non-CUPED limit matters, because adjusted numbers are usually the ones teams decide on. This is a vendor product post, so treat the single 52% figure as an illustration, not evidence of typical gains.

## Newly added today

**Core DS (5)**
- **Exact, narrow and fast: Use the Gaffke confidence interval for the mean of a discrete bounded variable.** Louis Abraham (arXiv stat.CO). *Statistical Modeling* · 3/5. The Gaffke interval extends Clopper–Pearson to ratings and scores. It is exact, now runs in under a millisecond, and ships in Python and R. For n ≤ 30 it is 17–20% shorter than betting intervals; Wald coverage drops as low as 6%.
- **Probability-Feasible Historical Transport: Sharp Bounds and Falsification Tests for Distributional Pre-Trend Designs.** University at Albany, SUNY (arXiv econ.EM). *Experimentation & Causal* · 3/5. Building distributional-DiD counterfactuals from mixtures of past gaps can produce "distributions" with negative probability, and pooling policy events can hide this. The paper gives sharp LP bounds and falsification tests.
- **Airport-level runway-incursion rates normalized by movements: hierarchical shrinkage estimates, ranking uncertainty and severity, FY2008–FY2024.** Single author (arXiv physics.soc-ph). *Statistical Modeling* · 3/5. A hierarchical negative-binomial model with an exposure offset flags 199 of 565 FAA airports as above the median. The ranking still shifts by about a third when exposure is redefined, and frequency barely predicts severity (ρ = −0.08).
- **Design-based causal inference for ROC curves** *(deepened from backlog)*. Tsinghua University (arXiv stat.ME). *Experimentation & Causal* · 3/5. Defines a causal ROC curve: the share of treated outcomes beating the control's top-α cutoff. Inference uses ranks only and comes covariate-adjusted, curve-wide and for the tail.
- **How we built Datadog Experiments** *(deepened from backlog; today's deep dive)*. Datadog Engineering Blog. *Experimentation & Causal* · 3/5. Segment and percentile CUPED via metric augmentation, plus Global lift = Coverage × Local lift.

**AI / ML-systems (2)**
- **Lessons from 12 Evals: Where They Break and What to Measure Instead.** Eugene Yan. *LLMs & GenAI* · 3/5. In eight of twelve public agent benchmarks the grader failed valid work, and some graders also passed hallucinated work. Yan argues for treating scores as noisy measurements of a model-and-harness pair and reporting pass^k instead of average pass rate.
- **Fact-Checking at Scale: Building a More Accurate and Efficient Synopsis Factuality System.** Netflix Tech Blog. *LLMs & GenAI* · 3/5. Separate literal and implicature judges, escalation of only low-confidence verdicts, and rechecks of the judges' own doubts. Recall on failing synopses rose from 0.77 to 0.86 at 52.4% lower cost. The results tables were images and are not captured.
