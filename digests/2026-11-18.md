## 🔎 Deep dive: Better Rankers, Worse Retrievers: The Hidden Cost of RL Post-Training in Generative Recommendation

*HKUST / Trip.com Group (arXiv cs.IR) · personalization-recsys (AI/ML-systems) · AI/ML pick (rotation: 2 of the last 4 deep dives were Core DS, so AI/ML-systems was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** Generative recommenders decode the semantic ID of a user's next item with a language model. The standard recipe is supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on logged next items, then reinforcement learning, usually GRPO. RL gains are reported within the first ten results. In the common deployment, though, the model is a *retrieval channel* that hands hundreds to thousands of candidates to a ranker: TSGR at Taobao passes 1,500, GrowthGR 1,000, OneRec and OneSearch 512. Even OneRec, an end-to-end system, did best as a candidate generator. Showing its own top results only matched the production cascade (+0.01% app stay time). Letting a reward model choose among its 512 candidates gave +0.54%. Benchmarks stop at K≤32, so RL's effect at retrieval depth had gone unmeasured.

**What they found.**
- **A better ranker and a worse retriever.** On Office, RL lifts NDCG@10 from 11.59% to 12.55%, but hit@50 falls from 25.73% to 24.22% and hit@500 from 52.84% to 47.69% (−5.1 points). The gain flips to a loss at K≈20 on Office and K≈50 on Industrial. Hit@K drops at every K≥100 on all five Amazon datasets, and 14–22% of the targets in the SFT model's top 500 fall out of it. The loss persists under beam search (Office hit@500 −3.06), while the standard protocol (beam 50 scored at K=10) shows only the gain (+0.39).
- **The objective isn't to blame.** Proposition 4.1 shows the exact KL-regularised RL solution can't lower expected hit@K at any K. Sharpening alone can't explain it either: entropy falls from 6.0 to 3.1 nats, but rank metrics ignore temperature. The loss needs new inversions of pairs the SFT model had ordered correctly.
- **The cause: the 15 negatives.** When the target is in a 16-item rollout, GRPO pushes the other 15 candidates down. These are the model's most plausible alternatives and often other users' next items. The penalties pile up per item and spread through shared SID tokens to users with similar histories. Prompts whose rollout misses the target (about 60% at the start on Office/Industrial) give no gradient to push back.
- **Causal tests.** Zeroing negative advantages removes the loss and the top-of-list gain. Keeping only negatives drops hit@500 by 19–21 points. DPO with negatives drawn from the SFT beam loses 4.1 points of hit@500, while random negatives leave it unchanged. A per-item "rollout record" computed *before* RL predicts who loses: prompts with the most negative records lose 27.4 points of hit@500 on Office.

**The fix: out-of-rollout anchoring.** Inside the rollout, GRPO is unchanged. One extra term, λ·KL(q₀‖q_θ) with λ=0.1, keeps the SFT model's relative scores for 16 items sampled outside the rollout. Those items are SID neighbours of the rollout candidates: half share its first two codes, half its first code. On Office this recovers 4.4 of the 5.1 lost points (hit@500 52.1% vs 47.7% under GRPO). On the other four datasets hit@500 returns to the SFT level or slightly above. NDCG@10 is statistically unchanged vs GRPO everywhere. Under REINFORCE++ the anchor adds 13.3 points of hit@500 on Office.

**Limits (the paper's).** Small catalogues (3–18k items), a Qwen2.5-1.5B backbone, one RL epoch, and RL hyper-parameters left untuned on three of the five datasets. There is no online test; the authors call industrial-scale validation with the ranker in the loop future work.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** The headline is a measurement lesson for any funnel: evaluate a stage at the depth the next stage consumes, or a "win" can ship a regression. The causal chain is unusually tight for recsys: a proposition rules out the objective, sign ablations follow, then a negative-sampling swap and a pre-RL predictor of the harmed users. The fix is cheap enough that SID-based retrieval teams running GRPO should simply try it, but the first step is adding hit@{100…1000} to the RL dashboard.
