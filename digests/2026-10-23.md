## 🔎 Deep dive: Lifetime-valid decisions by continually evolving AI analysts

*arXiv stat.ME (Sun, Zeng & Wang), Oct 2026 · experimentation-causal · novelty 4/5*
Why this pick: Core DS was due (only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS), and this is a solid Core-DS piece with an A/B-testing application.

🎙️ Deep-dive podcast: podcasts/2026-10-23-arxiv-lifetime-valid-continual-validation-ai-analysts.m4a (source: summary)
🎙️ Episode S2E12 — cover ✓, RSS draft ✓ (unpublished; publish from the RSS.com dashboard)

**Business context.** AI agents increasingly make decisions that can be framed as hypothesis tests (e.g. ship or don't ship on an A/B result). Classical tests control Type I error for a fixed procedure, but an AI analyst has internal randomness and keeps being updated; each update can add risk of approving a version whose error exceeds the target, and tuning against evaluation feedback can overfit.

**How it works.** "Continual validation": each proposed analyst version is tested on simulated data generated under the null before deployment; scenarios can be randomized and expanded over time. The authors prove that the lifetime probability of ever deploying a version with Type I error above the allowed level stays bounded regardless of how updates are chosen or how many are proposed, while sufficiently low-error updates pass with high probability. Demonstrated on evolving analysts for bioequivalence studies and product A/B tests.

**Impact (as reported).** Without continual validation Type I error reached 0.098 (bioequivalence) and 0.183 (A/B testing); with it, at most 0.045 and 0.044. Validated analysts kept 82% and 96% of the power of optimally selected valid updates.

**Claude's Take.** Novelty 4/5: treats the evolving AI analyst as the object whose error must be controlled over its lifetime. Caveats: abstract-level read; the guarantee is only as good as the null simulations.
