## 🔎 Deep dive: Do Similarity Measures Measure Similarity? How User Activity Creates Phantom Correlation in Recommender Systems

*arXiv cs.IR (Favier et al., University of Antwerp) · personalization-recsys · AI/ML pick (rotation: 2 of the last 4 deep dives were Core DS, so AI/ML-systems was due; chosen for its measurement/inference angle) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** Item-item similarity (lift, cosine, Pearson, learned EASE weights) underlies item-kNN, SLIM and EASE, on the assumption that co-occurrence means shared taste. But user activity is heavy-tailed. Toy case: 100 equally liked films, half the users watch each film with probability 0.01 and half with 0.20, all independently. Lift still shows films appearing together about 1.82× more often than chance. The authors call this *phantom correlation*.

**The model.** Three assumptions — independent consumption given an activity trait θ; activity raises every item's probability while preserving popularity order; basket size is a sufficient statistic for θ — are shown to be equivalent to a Rasch model: logit P(item i | θ) = h_i + θ, so a more active user has the odds of every item multiplied by the same factor. Consequences: total item-item covariance = Var(S) − Σ p_i(1−p_i) for any distribution; every pair is non-negatively correlated; lift is largest for rare items, and cosine/Pearson inherit a popularity-dependent baseline. The model is fitted by EM with a discrete distribution over activity levels; EASE is evaluated on the model's expected Gram matrix.

**Results.** On MovieLens-1M, 81% of item pairs have observed co-occurrence within a factor of two of what activity implies, against 35% under independence. Activity removes most of the mismatch of independence on MovieLens-1M/20M, about half on Amazon Books (5-core), and nothing on Last.fm (top-50 lists; history lengths vary less than under independence, so the assumptions fail). What activity does not explain: Spearman correlation between observed and modeled lift over pairs is only 0.31 on MovieLens-1M, and only the data contain negatively correlated pairs.

**What the authors do and don't claim.** Not that collaborative-filtering signals are fake; not a statistical test; not that activity is the only source of popularity bias. Whether the baseline biases final recommendations is future work.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** A Simpson's-paradox-style warning for anyone reading co-occurrence as affinity. The practical move is to benchmark similarity against an activity-only null and look at deviations. It is a diagnostic, not a new recommender; no downstream recommendation gains are shown.
