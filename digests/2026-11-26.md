## 🔎 Deep dive: Learning the Platform, Not the Learner: Sequencing Shortcuts in the Offline Evaluation of Educational Recommender Systems and LLM Rankers

*Youssef Jdidou (ENSAD / Hassan II University of Casablanca) · arXiv cs.IR, 24 Nov 2026 · personalization-recsys (AI/ML-systems) · AI/ML pick (rotation: 2 of the last 4 deep dives were Core DS, so AI/ML-systems was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** In educational logs the next activity is "seldom a free choice of the learner". Teachers assign problem sets, tutors draw problems until mastery, engines pick questions. The log is the output of a sequencing policy π. So when a next-activity recommender is trained and scored offline (rank the logged next activity high), it can win by imitating π rather than by finding what the learner needs. The paper calls this a shortcut that "is the design of the platform". Most of the public datasets were also collected for knowledge tracing, where sequencing didn't matter.

**The method.**
- **Three reportable numbers.** S_struct = the best score of any *structural* predictor (one that knows only the learner's last activity). Δ(f) = a model's score minus S_struct, the lift over structure. ρ = S_struct / best score of any method, the structural share. The author notes Δ still measures agreement with π, not pedagogical value.
- **Structural predictors.** Popularity, median course position, same-container continuation, and a first-order Markov chain (MC) on transitions. MC counts as structural because everyone with the same last activity gets the same ranking.
- **Ten personalised recommenders**, including ItemKNN, EASE, iALS, SKNN, knowledge-tracing policies (ZPD, DKT) and SASRec, on **8 datasets from 5 platforms**: four ASSISTments years, EdNet, Junyi, Eedi and a MOOC. NDCG@10 and HR@K over the full ranking, with bootstrap CIs and Holm-corrected paired tests. Leave-last-out and a global temporal split.
- **LLM rankers.** Qwen2.5-Instruct 0.5B–7B score 20-item candidate sets, with either random negatives or *structural* negatives drawn from MC's and the continuation baseline's top 20. A word-overlap baseline (LEX) is the control.
- **Simulation with known needs**, so offline NDCG can be compared with true usefulness (need@10) as the platform's share α of next-step choices grows.
- **Structure-corrected accuracy.** Test steps are reweighted by 1/max(p, 0.01), where p is MC's transition probability. This is a self-normalised IPS-style NDCG. The author stresses it "is not an estimate of online performance".

**Key results.**
- Structural share ρ is **0.81–1.00 (median 0.95)**. MC is the best structural predictor on 7 of 8 datasets. Without MC, the other structural baselines reach a median ρ of only 0.45: "The shortcut is the paths, not the syllabus as written."
- The best personalised model (always SASRec) is significantly better on 5 datasets (e.g. +0.102 on A15), significantly worse on A12 (−0.020), and not different on two. On Eedi the conclusion depends on the training seed.
- On transitions never seen in training, no method exceeds NDCG@10 0.162. On Junyi, learner-chosen exercises score 0.264 against 0.649 for platform suggestions.
- **LLM rankers:** with random negatives Qwen 0.5B reaches HR@1 0.600 on A09, about the same as LEX (0.594). With structural negatives they fall to 0.095–0.110 (A09) and 0.055 (EdNet). Going from 0.5B to 7B changes HR@1 by −0.03 to +0.06, with "no systematic gain".
- **Simulation:** as α rises to 0.9, the Kendall τ between NDCG@10 and true usefulness falls from 0.86 to 0.16. At α = 0.75 the exact-needs oracle drops to 7th of 11 by NDCG. Corrected accuracy keeps τ at 0.75 and ranks that oracle 2nd, though it also fails at α = 0.9.
- On real data, corrected lifts over structure are significant on 7 of 8 datasets (corrected ρ median 0.66).
- The paper ends with a six-point checklist: report structural baselines, S_struct, Δ with CIs, ρ and corrected accuracy; split results by step type and by who started the activity; use structural negatives; prefer temporal splits; don't read log agreement as learning value. The `beat-the-syllabus` package is released.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** Each ingredient is familiar: strong-baseline audits, sampled-metric pitfalls, IPS reweighting, and Han et al.'s transition-shortcut finding for sequential benchmarks. The new parts are a small set of reportable numbers (S_struct, Δ, ρ) and a simulation where offline NDCG ranks the true-needs oracle near the bottom. For data scientists the lesson goes beyond edtech. Any product where the platform chooses the next item (onboarding flows, autoplay, feeds) has the same problem: next-item accuracy partly scores your own logging policy. Reporting ρ against a Markov baseline, and running the "random negatives vs. structural negatives vs. LEX" check on any LLM-reranker claim, is cheap and revealing. Caveats: a single author, modest tuning grids, LLMs capped at 7B on CPU, and a corrected metric built on MC's own probabilities. That makes it a diagnostic, not a fair leaderboard. It does not replace randomised or online evaluation, which the paper says plainly.
