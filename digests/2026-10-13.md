## 🔬 Deep Dive: OneTrans-V2 — One Transformer for Retrieval, Pre-rank and Fine-rank

**Source:** [arXiv cs.IR (Cao et al.)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28589) · Published 2026-09 · Category: Personalization & Recsys (AI/ML-systems)

*(AI/ML-systems was due in the rotation: the last 4 deep dives included 2 Core DS.)*

🎙️ Deep-dive podcast: podcasts/2026-10-13-arxiv-onetrans-v2-unified-transformer-recsys.m4a
🎙️ Episode S1E37 — cover ✓, RSS draft ✓ (unpublished; publish from the RSS.com dashboard)

**Business.** Industrial recommenders run a cascade of retrieval, pre-rank and fine-rank, each a separate model. That repeats user-sequence encoding, isolates optimisation and duplicates engineering.

**Technical.** One Transformer serves all three stages. The user behaviour sequence is encoded once as shared context while stage-specific candidate features are kept. Stages train jointly with in-model distillation from fine-rank to pre-rank; the backbone scales with sparse mixture-of-experts and μP-style parameterisation. Decision-Conditioned Generative Retrieval (DCGR) predicts a decision prefix and generates items conditioned on it; Sequence-Native Training (SNT) amortises lifelong-sequence encoding across exposures. Details beyond the abstract were not reviewed.

**Impact.** Realized (abstract): +9.74% GMV in production and 3.2x throughput versus the separate per-stage models on the same hardware. Platform, baseline and A/B design are not given.

**Claude's Take (4/5).** Serving the whole cascade from one model is credible and the throughput claim matters as much as the GMV claim, but +9.74% GMV without baseline or experiment detail should be read as a claim. With jointly trained stages, online experiments, not per-stage offline metrics, must carry the evidence.
