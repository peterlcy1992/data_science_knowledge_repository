## 🔎 Deep dive: When Can You Ship on Evals Alone? Trial-Level Surrogacy for Offline Evaluation of LLM Systems

*arXiv stat.ME (Mårten Schultzberg, Spotify) · experimentation-causal · Core DS pick (rotation: only 1 of the last 4 deep dives was Core DS, so Core DS was due) · novelty 4/5 · full text read*

**The problem.** Showing that an offline eval (e.g. an LLM judge) agrees with human judgment does not tell a team which changes can ship without an A/B test. That depends on whether eval treatment effects predict online treatment effects across a class of comparable interventions: *trial-level surrogacy*, which the paper shows is logically independent of unit-level (Prentice) surrogacy.

**The method.** Each intervention is a unit with a true eval effect and a true online effect. In launch logs, sampling noise in both estimates attenuates the observed relationship; under a bivariate normal working model, subtracting the reported within-intervention variances recovers the between-intervention covariance. The corrected moments give a closed-form ship/test/kill rule that bounds the probability of shipping a change with a non-positive online effect. LLM judges add a separate distortion: equal accuracy on both arms only rescales eval effects, but arm-dependent accuracy can reverse their sign. Pooling heterogeneous intervention classes can manufacture apparent surrogacy. Once a gate ships on evals alone, the eval-to-outcome relationship is no longer observable in the ship and kill regions, so randomized audit experiments are needed to detect drift.

**Results.** On 183 interventions from the Upworthy Research Archive (a GPT-4o-mini judge of headline informativeness vs click-through), reliability ratios are 0.93 (eval) and 0.74 (outcome), the pooled corrected R² is 0.023, per-class bootstrap intervals cover zero, and the fitted shipping threshold exceeds the largest possible eval delta, so every intervention is sent to an A/B test. The authors describe this as intended fail-closed behaviour and note a better-matched eval could behave differently.

**Claude's Take (opinion).** Brings the clinical surrogate-endpoint literature to the question every LLM-product team faces. Evidence is simulation plus one deliberately mismatched public illustration, so it shows the machinery works and fails closed, not that a good eval will clear the gate in practice.
