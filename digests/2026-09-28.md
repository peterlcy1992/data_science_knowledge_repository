## 🔬 Deep Dive: Robust A/B Decisions

**Source:** [arXiv (Farrell, Korganbekova, Misra)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07633) · Published 2026-09 · Category: Experimentation & Causal Inference

Max Farrell, Malika Korganbekova, and Sanjog Misra start from an uncomfortable observation: the standard "run the A/B test, ship the arm with the significant t-test" workflow answers the wrong question. A hypothesis test tells you whether a difference is *detectable*; it says nothing about whether shipping that arm is actually the right *economic decision* once you account for how fragile the estimated advantage might be once real-world conditions drift even slightly from what the experiment measured.

**The idea.** The authors reframe deployment as a robust decision-theory problem. Instead of scoring each arm only by its point estimate, they evaluate it by an "ambiguity-penalized value" — how good the arm looks not just under the exact outcome distribution the experiment measured, but across a neighborhood of distributions plausibly close to it. Arms whose apparent edge evaporates under small perturbations get penalized; arms with a genuinely robust advantage don't. The technical trick that makes this practical is applying the Donsker-Varadhan variational representation, which turns what could have been an intractable search over a whole neighborhood of distributions into a simple closed-form formula. The result needs nothing beyond what a standard A/B test already produces, plus one interpretable "trust" parameter that dials how ambiguity-averse the decision should be.

**The validation.** This isn't just a theory paper — the authors test the rule against **552 real digital-advertising experiments** from a major US online platform, and find it substantially reduces regret (the gap versus the best-in-hindsight choice) compared to conventional significance-based deployment.

**Why it matters.** Because the rule slots into the same pipeline as an ordinary t-test — same inputs, one extra parameter, closed-form — any team currently gating ship decisions on p-values or Bayesian win-probability thresholds could adopt it without new instrumentation. That combination of theoretical grounding, practical simplicity, and large-scale real-world validation is what separates this from the many "significance testing is the wrong criterion" critiques that never produce a usable alternative.
